MCQ Practice Questions
Q1: Anki #2
The osmotic threshold for ADH secretion occurs at approximately 280-285 mOsm/kg, while thirst is triggered at 290-295 mOsm/kg. What is the physiological advantage of this separation?
A ADH maximizes water reabsorption before behavioral responses to thirst occur
B Thirst must occur first to stimulate ADH release
C ADH only works in extreme dehydration
D Thirst and ADH thresholds should always be identical
Explanation:
The staggered thresholds allow ADH to work as the first-line defense, conserving water through renal mechanisms before thirst-driven drinking behavior. This is more efficient than waiting for behavioral responses.
Q2: Anki #6
During labor, the positive feedback loop driving uterine contractions is mediated by which reflex?
A Ferguson reflex: cervical distension → ↑oxytocin → ↑contractions
B Bainbridge reflex: increased uterine stretch → ↓heart rate
C Milk letdown reflex: suckling → ↑prolactin
D Moro reflex: abrupt disturbance → startle response
Explanation:
The Ferguson reflex is the oxytocin-mediated positive feedback during labor. It amplifies contractions as cervical dilation increases, ensuring labor progresses efficiently.
Q3: Anki #81-82
A 58-year-old man with hypertension is given vasopressin (ADH). Which tissue would respond most dramatically to vasopressin via V₁a receptors?
A Vascular smooth muscle → vasoconstriction
B Renal collecting duct → water reabsorption
C Anterior pituitary → ACTH release
D Adrenal cortex → aldosterone synthesis
Explanation:
V₁a receptors on vascular smooth muscle mediate vasoconstriction via Gq → IP₃ → ↑Ca²⁺. This is why high-dose vasopressin can cause hypertensive crisis and is used in cardiac arrest protocols.
Q4: Anki #85
A 42-year-old woman with a pituitary adenoma develops polyuria (large urine volume) and polydipsia. Which finding would distinguish central from nephrogenic diabetes insipidus?
A Administration of desmopressin → decreased urine output (suggests central DI)
B High urine osmolality despite low plasma osmolality
C Low plasma ADH levels
D Dehydration increases urine output
Explanation:
The desmopressin (DDAVP) challenge test is diagnostic: central DI responds (urine output decreases, osmolality increases) because kidneys CAN respond to V₂ agonists; nephrogenic DI does not respond because kidneys cannot respond to any ADH.
Q5: Anki #90-92
Oxytocin and vasopressin (ADH) are both 9-amino acid peptides that differ at 2 positions. Despite their structural similarity, they have completely different physiological effects. Why?
A They activate different G-protein coupled receptors with different downstream signaling cascades
B Small peptides cannot share any receptor specificity
C Oxytocin and ADH are metabolized differently in the liver
D The brain produces oxytocin while kidneys produce ADH
Explanation:
Oxytocin receptors couple to Gq → IP₃/DAG (uterine contraction, milk ejection), while V₂ receptors couple to Gs → cAMP (water reabsorption). Distinct receptors and signaling pathways account for the different effects despite structural similarity.
Q6: Anki #88
A 35-year-old man with small cell lung cancer develops SIADH (Syndrome of Inappropriate ADH). Lab findings show plasma [Na⁺] = 122 mEq/L (normal 135-145) and urine osmolality 450 mOsm/kg (high). What is the pathophysiology?
A Excessive ADH secretion → ↑water reabsorption → dilutional hyponatremia with concentrated urine
B Adrenal insufficiency reducing aldosterone → sodium loss
C Nephrogenic ADH resistance → inability to concentrate urine
D Excessive free water loss through diarrhea
Explanation:
SIADH is characterized by inappropriately HIGH ADH causing excessive water reabsorption. The urine remains concentrated (high osmolality) while plasma becomes dilute (hyponatremia). Small cell lung cancer is a classic paraneoplastic cause of SIADH.
Clinical Vignettes
Vignette 1: Central Diabetes Insipidus
A 52-year-old man presents with sudden-onset polyuria (5L/day) and severe polydipsia after head trauma during a motor vehicle accident. His plasma osmolality is 312 mOsm/kg, but his urine osmolality is only 85 mOsm/kg (should be ≥600 mOsm/kg if dehydrated). Plasma ADH is low (0.5 pg/mL, normal 1-5).
Diagnosis: Central diabetes insipidus (head trauma → ↓ADH production from posterior pituitary)
Treatment: Desmopressin/DDAVP intranasal or subcutaneous (V₂-selective agonist)
Expected response: Urine output ↓, osmolality ↑ to 400-600 mOsm/kg
Vignette 2: Nephrogenic DI from Lithium
A 38-year-old woman on chronic lithium for bipolar disorder presents with polyuria and polydipsia (drinking 4L water daily). Plasma osmolality is 305 mOsm/kg with elevated ADH (8.2 pg/mL), but urine osmolality is only 180 mOsm/kg. The desmopressin challenge test shows minimal improvement in urine osmolality.
Diagnosis: Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus from lithium toxicity (damages collecting duct V₂ receptors and aquaporin-2)
Distinguishing feature: HIGH ADH despite polyuria (kidney is unresponsive)
Treatment: Discontinue lithium, thiazide diuretics (paradoxically ↓urine output), low-sodium diet, NSAIDs
Vignette 3: SIADH
A 62-year-old man with recently diagnosed small cell lung cancer presents with confusion, headache, and seizure. Serum [Na⁺] = 118 mEq/L (severe hyponatremia), plasma osmolality 245 mOsm/kg, and urine osmolality 380 mOsm/kg (inappropriately high). Urine sodium is elevated at 92 mEq/L.
Diagnosis: SIADH from paraneoplastic ADH secretion by lung cancer cells
Pathophysiology: Excessive ADH → ↑water reabsorption → dilutional hyponatremia with concentrated urine (inappropriate for plasma osmolality)
Treatment: Fluid restriction (first-line), saline in severe symptomatic hyponatremia (seizures, coma), V₂ antagonists (vaptans) in refractory cases
Vignette 4: Oxytocin and Labor
A 28-year-old primigravida at 40 weeks gestation is admitted with mild contractions (every 10 minutes). After 6 hours of slow labor, an oxytocin infusion is started. Within 30 minutes, contractions increase to every 2-3 minutes with increased intensity. The cervix dilates from 3 cm to 6 cm over 2 hours.
Mechanism: Oxytocin acts on uterine Gq receptors → IP₃ → ↑Ca²⁺ → muscle contraction
Ferguson reflex: Increased cervical distension from labor → afferent signals → ↑endogenous oxytocin → positive feedback amplification
Clinical note: Oxytocin receptor upregulation throughout pregnancy allows increasing responsiveness to oxytocin as parturition approaches
Vignette 5: Lactation Failure
A 31-year-old postpartum mother reports milk ejection reflex triggered by her baby's cry (even before nursing). Physical exam shows brisk nipple erection. Serum prolactin is elevated. However, the milk supply has been low, and she's considering metoclopramide (dopamine antagonist).
Milk ejection: Conditioned oxytocin release (via baby's cry) stimulates myoepithelial Gs receptors → cAMP → milk ejection
Milk production (galactopoiesis): Requires prolactin (tonic, under dopamine inhibition)
Metoclopramide mechanism: Dopamine antagonist → ↓dopamine inhibition of prolactin → ↑prolactin → ↑milk production (acts as galactogogue)
Common Test Traps & Misconceptions
TRAP 1: ADH vs. Thirst Thresholds
Myth:
Thirst and ADH secretion are triggered at the same plasma osmolality.
Truth:
ADH secretion begins at ~280-285 mOsm/kg, but thirst is not felt until ~290-295 mOsm/kg (5-10 mOsm/kg higher). This allows ADH to maximize water conservation before the behavioral response of drinking.
TRAP 2: SIADH Urine Osmolality
Myth:
In SIADH, urine osmolality is low because ADH suppresses urine concentration.
Truth:
In SIADH, urine osmolality is INAPPROPRIATELY HIGH relative to plasma osmolality. The kidneys are over-concentrating urine due to excessive ADH, causing dilutional hyponatremia (not a concentrating defect).
TRAP 3: Nephrogenic DI ADH Levels
Myth:
In nephrogenic DI, ADH levels are low (like in central DI).
Truth:
In nephrogenic DI, ADH levels are HIGH or NORMAL-to-HIGH (because the kidney cannot respond, the body produces more ADH in a failed attempt to increase water reabsorption). Low ADH = central DI. High ADH + polyuria = nephrogenic DI.
TRAP 4: Desmopressin Efficacy
Myth:
Desmopressin (DDAVP) works in all forms of diabetes insipidus.
Truth:
Desmopressin is V₂-selective and works in central DI (kidney collecting ducts are intact). It does NOT work in nephrogenic DI (kidney collecting ducts cannot respond to any ADH). In gestational DI, desmopressin works because it's resistant to placental vasopressinase (native ADH is degraded).
TRAP 5: Oxytocin Receptor Antagonists
Myth:
Oxytocin receptor antagonists (atosiban) are highly effective at stopping premature labor.
Truth:
Oxytocin receptor antagonists are INEFFECTIVE because oxytocin receptors upregulate throughout pregnancy, increasing sensitivity. Blocking receptors in late pregnancy (when already upregulated) cannot overcome the cascade. Blocking earlier in pregnancy would be more effective, but this isn't clinically done.
TRAP 6: Oxytocin vs. Prolactin
Myth:
Oxytocin produces milk; prolactin ejects it.
Truth:
OPPOSITE: Prolactin stimulates MILK PRODUCTION (galactopoiesis); oxytocin causes MILK EJECTION. Oxytocin is the acute response to suckling/nipple stimulation. Prolactin is the chronic response (over days/weeks).
TRAP 7: Cold Water and ADH
Myth:
Drinking cold water increases ADH secretion due to the osmotic load.
Truth:
Cold-sensitive oropharyngeal receptors INHIBIT ADH release when activated by cold water (anticipatory response before osmolality actually increases). This prevents over-hydration. It's an exception to normal osmoreceptor-driven ADH regulation.
TRAP 8: Baroreceptor Sensitivity
Myth:
Small decreases in blood pressure (1-2%) immediately increase ADH.
Truth:
Baroreceptor-mediated ADH secretion requires >8-10% blood volume loss (significant hypotension). The relationship is exponential (steep at large volume losses). Small volume changes primarily trigger thirst and sympathetic activation, not ADH. Osmoreceptors are more sensitive than baroreceptors for ADH.
TRAP 9: V₁a vs. V₂ Signaling
Myth:
V₁a and V₂ receptors both couple to Gs → cAMP signaling.
Truth:
V₁a couples to Gq → IP₃/DAG → ↑Ca²⁺ (vasoconstriction, liver glycogenolysis). V₂ couples to Gs → cAMP → PKA → aquaporin-2 insertion (water reabsorption). Different receptors, different signaling, different tissues.
TRAP 10: Stress and Reproduction
Myth:
Stress increases GnRH secretion (explaining why stressed athletes lose menses).
Truth:
Stress increases cortisol → facilitates GnIH (gonadotropin-inhibiting hormone) → suppresses GnRH → ↓LH/FSH → amenorrhea. This is an energetic trade-off: the body prioritizes stress responses over reproduction under adverse conditions.
