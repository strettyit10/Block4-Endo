BRS Physiology Ch. 7
Q1
A 25-year-old woman undergoes MRI for headaches. Imaging shows a craniopharyngioma compressing the pituitary stalk. Which of the following effects on hormone levels would be expected?
A) Increased GH, decreased prolactin
B) Increased prolactin, decreased LH/FSH/TSH/GH
C) Decreased prolactin, increased TSH
D) Increased ACTH, decreased prolactin
E) Normal prolactin, decreased TSH only
EXPLANATION
The pituitary stalk carries dopamine (prolactin-inhibiting factor) from the hypothalamus to the anterior pituitary. Stalk compression → loss of dopamine → hyperprolactinemia. Simultaneously, all other hypothalamic releasing hormones (GHRH, TRH, CRH, GnRH) are also lost → decreased GH, TSH, LH/FSH, ACTH. The stalk effect classically produces: high prolactin + low everything else.
TEACHING POINT
Stalk section = high prolactin + low all other anterior pituitary hormones. This is opposite of a prolactinoma (which also has high prolactin but suppresses everything else via a different mechanism).
Q2
Which hypothalamic nucleus contains magnocellular neurons that synthesize vasopressin and oxytocin?
A) Arcuate nucleus and ventromedial nucleus
B) Paraventricular nucleus and supraoptic nucleus
C) Suprachiasmatic nucleus and preoptic area
D) Dorsomedial nucleus and lateral hypothalamus
E) Periventricular nucleus and zona incerta
EXPLANATION
Magnocellular neurons in the paraventricular nucleus (PVN) and supraoptic nucleus (SON) synthesize vasopressin (ADH) and oxytocin as prohormones → transported down axons to posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis) for release. Parvocellular neurons in the PVN produce CRH. The arcuate nucleus produces GHRH, kisspeptin, and dopamine.
TEACHING POINT
PVN + SON → magnocellular → ADH and OT → posterior pituitary. Arcuate → parvocellular → GHRH and dopamine → anterior pituitary (via portal).
Q3
In the hypothalamic-pituitary portal system, the primary plexus collects releasing hormones and the secondary plexus delivers them to the anterior pituitary. Destruction of portal vessels would have what effect?
A) Increased secretion of all anterior pituitary hormones
B) Selective decrease in TSH only
C) Decreased secretion of GH, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH with increased prolactin
D) Decreased prolactin with normal other hormones
E) No significant hormonal effect
EXPLANATION
The portal system carries both releasing hormones AND dopamine (the primary prolactin inhibitor). Destruction removes releasing hormones → ↓GH, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH. But it also removes dopamine → disinhibition of prolactin → ↑prolactin. This pattern (pan-hypopituitarism + hyperprolactinemia) is the hallmark of stalk disruption.
TEACHING POINT
Portal destruction = lost releasing hormones + lost dopamine. Net: all anterior hormones ↓ EXCEPT prolactin ↑.
Q4
A medical student reads that anterior pituitary cells include 'acidophils' and 'basophils.' Which hormone-secreting cells are classified as acidophils?
A) Corticotrophs and thyrotrophs
B) Gonadotrophs and corticotrophs
C) Somatotrophs and lactotrophs
D) Thyrotrophs and gonadotrophs
E) All five cell types stain as acidophils
EXPLANATION
Anterior pituitary staining: Acidophils (stain pink with eosin): Somatotrophs (GH) and Lactotrophs (prolactin) — the "S.L." cells. Basophils (stain blue with hematoxylin): Thyrotrophs (TSH), Gonadotrophs (LH/FSH), Corticotrophs (ACTH/POMC) — the "T.G.C." cells. Chromophobes: agranular, poor staining — often null cells or degranulated cells.
TEACHING POINT
Mnemonic: FLAT PiG → FSH+LH (Gonadotrophs), ACTH (Corticotrophs), TSH (Thyrotrophs) = basophils. GH (Somatotrophs), Prolactin (Lactotrophs) = acidophils.
Q5
A 35-year-old woman with Sheehan syndrome (postpartum pituitary necrosis) would most predictably have which hormonal pattern?
A) Increased prolactin and decreased TSH
B) Decreased prolactin and decreased TSH/LH/FSH/GH/ACTH
C) Isolated prolactin deficiency
D) Increased GH and TSH
E) Decreased ACTH and TSH with preserved LH/FSH
EXPLANATION
Sheehan syndrome: ischemic necrosis of anterior pituitary from postpartum hemorrhage/shock → pan-hypopituitarism. Loss of lactotrophs → can't breastfeed (failure to lactate is often the presenting symptom). Loss of all other anterior pituitary cells → ↓ TSH, ACTH, LH/FSH, GH. Prolactin is LOW (opposite of stalk compression). Posterior pituitary is typically SPARED (separate blood supply from inferior hypophyseal arteries).
TEACHING POINT
Sheehan = pan-hypopituitarism including LOW prolactin (failure to lactate). Stalk compression = high prolactin. Key distinguishing feature.
Q6
Which of the following best describes the feedback control in the hypothalamic-pituitary axis?
A) Short-loop: end-organ hormone acts on hypothalamus and pituitary
B) Long-loop: end-organ hormone acts on hypothalamus and pituitary
C) Ultrashort-loop: pituitary hormone acts back on hypothalamus
D) Short-loop: anterior pituitary hormone acts on hypothalamus
E) All loops: end-organ, pituitary, and hypothalamic hormone feedback simultaneously
EXPLANATION
The HPx axis has THREE feedback loops: (1) Long-loop: end-organ hormone (e.g., cortisol, T4, estrogen) → acts on hypothalamus AND pituitary. (2) Short-loop: anterior pituitary hormone (e.g., ACTH, TSH) → acts back on hypothalamus. (3) Ultrashort-loop: hypothalamic hormone (e.g., CRH) → acts on hypothalamic neurons itself (autofeedback). All three operate simultaneously.
TEACHING POINT
Long loop = most clinically relevant. Short and ultrashort loops = fine-tuning. All are negative feedback EXCEPT mid-cycle estrogen surge (positive feedback → LH surge).
Q7
Empty sella syndrome occurs when CSF fills the sella turcica, compressing the pituitary. Which finding is most expected?
A) Panhypopituitarism in all cases
B) Hyperprolactinemia with pan-hypopituitarism
C) Usually normal pituitary function; prolactin may be mildly elevated
D) Isolated ADH deficiency
E) Elevated GH and IGF-1
EXPLANATION
In primary empty sella, the pituitary gland is flattened against the sella wall but retains enough functional tissue that pituitary function is USUALLY NORMAL. Mild hyperprolactinemia may occur if the pituitary stalk is distorted. Secondary empty sella (post-surgery, radiation, apoplexy) is more likely to cause hypopituitarism. The key teaching point: empty sella appearance ≠ hypopituitarism.
TEACHING POINT
Primary empty sella: normal hormone function typically. Secondary empty sella (post-trauma/surgery): may have hypopituitarism. Do not assume hypopituitarism from imaging alone.
Q8
Kisspeptin neurons project to GnRH neurons. What is the primary function of kisspeptin in the HPG axis?
A) It inhibits GnRH release to prevent premature puberty
B) It is the primary stimulator of GnRH release, acting as a gate for puberty onset and pulsatile LH secretion
C) It directly stimulates LH secretion from gonadotrophs
D) It inhibits dopamine release in the tuberoinfundibular pathway
E) It stimulates somatotroph growth hormone secretion
EXPLANATION
Kisspeptin (KISS1 gene product) from KNDy neurons in the arcuate nucleus is the master stimulator of GnRH release. Kisspeptin → GnRH → LH/FSH. Kisspeptin is essential for puberty onset — kisspeptin or kisspeptin receptor mutations → Kallmann-like hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. Kisspeptin also mediates positive (mid-cycle estrogen → kisspeptin surge) and negative feedback. Kisspeptin receptor antagonists suppress GnRH → used in endometriosis.
TEACHING POINT
Kisspeptin = gatekeeper of GnRH. Loss of kisspeptin receptor = pubertal failure. Estrogen positive feedback → kisspeptin surge → GnRH surge → LH surge.
Q9
A 22-year-old male presents with anosmia and inability to initiate puberty. GnRH stimulation test shows an appropriate LH and FSH response. What is the diagnosis?
A) Kallmann syndrome — GnRH neuron migration failure
B) Primary hypogonadism
C) Pituitary adenoma causing hypogonadotropic hypogonadism
D) Constitutional delay of puberty
E) Klinefelter syndrome
EXPLANATION
Kallmann syndrome: failure of GnRH neuron migration from olfactory placode to hypothalamus (GnRH neurons originate in the nose!) — linked to KAL1 (X-linked), FGFR1, PROK2 mutations. Features: anosmia (olfactory bulb hypoplasia) + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (low LH/FSH/testosterone). GnRH stimulation test: LH/FSH RISE (pituitary is intact) — this distinguishes Kallmann from pituitary failure. Treatment: pulsatile GnRH (Gonadorelin) or gonadotropins for fertility.
TEACHING POINT
Kallmann = anosmia + HH + normal pituitary (GnRH stim test normal). GnRH neurons migrate with olfactory neurons — both fail to migrate in Kallmann.
Q10
A patient with a pituitary macroadenoma (non-functional) develops bitemporal hemianopia. Which anatomical structure is compressed?
A) Optic tract
B) Optic chiasm
C) Optic nerve
D) Meyer\'s loop
E) Lateral geniculate nucleus
EXPLANATION
The optic chiasm sits directly above the pituitary gland. Upward extension of a pituitary macroadenoma compresses the crossing nasal fibers at the chiasm → loss of temporal visual fields bilaterally = bitemporal hemianopia. This is a classic examination finding that should prompt pituitary imaging. The non-crossing temporal fibers are preserved → nasal visual fields intact.
TEACHING POINT
Pituitary tumor → compresses optic chiasm → nasal fibers cross here → lose temporal fields bilaterally. "Bitemporal hemianopia = think pituitary."
Q1
Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) exerts its effects on somatotrophs by which mechanism?
A) Binding to a receptor coupled to Gi, decreasing cAMP
B) Binding to a tyrosine kinase receptor, activating STAT5
C) Binding to a receptor coupled to Gs, increasing cAMP and activating PKA
D) Binding to a nuclear receptor and directly regulating transcription
E) Binding to a receptor coupled to Gq, increasing IP3 and DAG
EXPLANATION
GHRH binds a GPCR on somatotrophs coupled to Gs → adenylyl cyclase ↑ → cAMP ↑ → PKA activation → GH gene transcription + GH secretion. This is the same mechanism used by many pituitary releasing hormones (CRH → corticotrophs via Gs; TRH → thyrotrophs via Gq). Somatostatin binds Gi-coupled GPCR → ↓cAMP → inhibits GH secretion.
TEACHING POINT
GHRH → Gs → ↑cAMP → GH release. Somatostatin → Gi → ↓cAMP → GH inhibition. Gi vs Gs = key opposition.
Q2
A woman is found to have a prolactinoma. Which of the following is the first-line treatment?
A) Transsphenoidal surgery
B) Radiation therapy
C) Cabergoline (dopamine agonist)
D) Octreotide
E) Bromocriptine only for microadenomas
EXPLANATION
Prolactinomas are unique among pituitary adenomas — medical therapy with dopamine agonists (cabergoline preferred, bromocriptine alternative) is FIRST-LINE, even for macroadenomas causing visual field defects. Dopamine agonists restore dopamine signaling → reduce prolactin secretion AND shrink the tumor (~80-90% respond). Surgery is reserved for medical failure, intolerance, or preference. This is the key exception to the "surgery first" rule for pituitary adenomas.
TEACHING POINT
Prolactinoma exception: dopamine agonist FIRST (not surgery). Cabergoline preferred over bromocriptine (longer acting, fewer side effects, better tolerated).
Q3
Somatostatin is produced in the hypothalamic periventricular nucleus and inhibits GH secretion. Which additional anterior pituitary hormone does somatostatin also inhibit?
A) ACTH
B) LH
C) TSH
D) Prolactin
E) FSH
EXPLANATION
Somatostatin inhibits both GH and TSH secretion from the anterior pituitary. This dual inhibitory action is the basis for octreotide's use in TSH-secreting adenomas. Somatostatin does not significantly inhibit ACTH, LH, FSH, or prolactin at physiologic concentrations. TRH, in contrast, stimulates both TSH AND prolactin.
TEACHING POINT
Somatostatin inhibits GH + TSH. TRH stimulates TSH + prolactin. These dual actions are clinically important.
Q4
Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) from the hypothalamus acts on corticotrophs to stimulate ACTH secretion. What is the primary second messenger system involved?
A) Gq → PLC → IP3/DAG
B) Gs → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA
C) Gi → ↓adenylyl cyclase
D) Tyrosine kinase → JAK-STAT
E) cGMP via guanylyl cyclase
EXPLANATION
CRH binds CRHR1 on corticotrophs → Gs-coupled → ↑adenylyl cyclase → ↑cAMP → PKA activation → POMC gene transcription → ACTH processing and release. This is the same Gs mechanism as GHRH (for GH). Importantly, CRH also activates PKC, but the primary second messenger is cAMP.
TEACHING POINT
CRH → Gs → cAMP → PKA → POMC → ACTH. Most hypothalamic releasing hormones use either Gs (↑cAMP) or Gq (↑IP3/DAG).
Q5
During surgery, a patient has a pituitary adenoma resected. Post-operatively, she develops polyuria with dilute urine and normal serum sodium (early). What is the most likely explanation?
A) SIADH from surgical stress
B) Damage to posterior pituitary or stalk causing central DI
C) Hypothyroidism from anterior pituitary damage
D) Adrenal insufficiency from ACTH deficiency
E) Diabetes mellitus from stress hyperglycemia
EXPLANATION
Central DI after pituitary surgery: disruption of the posterior pituitary or stalk → loss of ADH → inability to concentrate urine → polyuria with dilute urine, rising serum sodium (if patient cannot drink enough). Triphasic response: (1) DI (ADH storage lost) → (2) SIADH (stored ADH released from dying neurons) → (3) Permanent DI. Treatment: desmopressin (DDAVP).
TEACHING POINT
Post-pituitary surgery: watch for DI (polyuria, dilute urine, hypernatremia). May have triphasic response. Treat central DI with desmopressin.
