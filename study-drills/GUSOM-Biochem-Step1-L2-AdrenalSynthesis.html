Biochem L2: Adrenal Synthesis

USMLE Step 1 · CAH patterns · Pheochromocytoma · MEN syndromes · Cushing's workup

High-Yield Step 1 Concepts
Adrenal Zones
What is the GFR/ACE mnemonic for adrenal zones?
Answer
Glomerulosa → Aldosterone · Fasciculata → Cortisol · Reticularis → Estrogen/Androgens
Steroid Synthesis
What is the rate-limiting step in adrenal steroid synthesis?
Answer
Cholesterol → Pregnenolone via StAR (steroidogenic acute regulatory protein) + CYP11A1 (P450scc). StAR is rate-limiting—ACTH ↑ StAR expression.
21-Hydroxylase Deficiency
Why is 21-OH deficiency the most common CAH (90-95%)?
Answer
21-hydroxylase is the final step for both cortisol AND aldosterone synthesis. Loss → ↓cortisol, ↓aldosterone (salt-wasting), ↑17-OHP (marker), virilization in females (46,XX).
11β-Hydroxylase Deficiency
Why does 11β-OH deficiency cause hypertension, not hypotension?
Answer
11-deoxycorticosterone (DOC) accumulates upstream of the block. DOC is a potent mineralocorticoid → HTN + hypokalemia. Also ↑androgens → virilization. Key: ↑17-OHP distinguishes from Conn's.
17α-Hydroxylase Deficiency
Why does 17α-OH deficiency cause feminization in 46,XY individuals?
Answer
17α-hydroxylase is the gateway to BOTH cortisol AND androgen synthesis. Loss → ↓testosterone (Wolffian duct regression), ↓DHT (female external genitalia), ↑mineralocorticoids (HTN, hypokalemia).
3β-HSD Deficiency
What steroids are affected in 3β-HSD deficiency? Why the unusual virilization pattern?
Answer
Early enzyme → all steroid pathways blocked. ↑DHEA (weak androgen) → mild female virilization; salt-wasting. 46,XY with ambiguous genitalia (testosterone ↓). Rare form of CAH.
Lipoid CAH
Why is StAR/CYP11A1 (lipoid CAH) the most severe form?
Answer
Very first step → NO steroids made. Lipid droplets accumulate in adrenal. 46,XY → phenotypic female (no testosterone). Always salt-wasting + adrenal failure. Most severe form.
Cortisol Regulation
What are the two diagnostic cortisol screening tests for Cushing's syndrome?
Answer
24-hour urinary free cortisol (UFC) and late-night salivary cortisol (loss of normal diurnal rhythm). 1mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test (DST) if screening abnormal.
High-Dose DST
How does high-dose DST differentiate pituitary from ectopic Cushing's?
Answer
Pituitary (70%): Cortisol suppresses by ≥50% (responds to high-dose dexamethasone feedback). Ectopic/Adrenal: NO suppression (autonomous secretion).
Cushing's Disease vs Syndrome
Distinguish Cushing's disease from Cushing's syndrome.
Answer
Cushing's disease = pituitary ACTH secretion (70%). Cushing's syndrome = any hypercortisolism (includes adrenal, ectopic ACTH, pituitary).
Addison's Disease
What are the two most common etiologies of Addison's disease (primary AI)?
Answer
Worldwide: TB (adrenal destruction). US: Autoimmune (most common). Key finding: ↑ACTH + ↓cortisol + hyperpigmentation + hyponatremia + hyperkalemia.
Nelson's Syndrome
What is Nelson's syndrome and when does it occur?
Answer
Post-bilateral adrenalectomy for Cushing's disease: loss of cortisol feedback → ACTH-secreting pituitary adenoma enlarges unopposed → hyperpigmentation + visual field defects. Pituitary tumor complications.
Waterhouse-Friderichsen
What organism and pathophysiology underlie Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome?
Answer
Neisseria meningitidis sepsis → bilateral adrenal hemorrhage → acute adrenal insufficiency → shock + DIC + purpuric rash. Medical emergency.
Conn's Syndrome
How do you screen for Conn's syndrome (primary hyperaldosteronism)?
Answer
Aldosterone:renin ratio >20-30 on 12-hour upright positioning. Differentiator from secondary hyperaldosteronism (↑renin = renal artery stenosis, CHF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome).
Adrenal Androgens
What is the adrenal androgen marker and what does adrenarche represent?
Answer
DHEA-S (dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate) is the most abundant adrenal androgen. Adrenarche = pubertal rise of adrenal androgens (independent of gonadal hormones). Key in PCOS vs late-onset CAH differentiation.
PCOS vs Late-Onset CAH
How do you distinguish PCOS from late-onset 21-OH CAH?
Answer
17-OHP level: CAH (↑↑17-OHP; diagnostic), PCOS (normal 17-OHP). Both: hirsutism, anovulation, elevated androgens, insulin resistance. CAH requires treatment; PCOS does not.
Pheochromocytoma
What is the classic triad of pheochromocytoma symptoms?
Answer
Episodic severe HTN, diaphoresis, headache. Also palpitations, chest/abdominal pain. "Rule of 10s": 10% bilateral, 10% malignant, 10% extra-adrenal, 10% pediatric.
Pheochromocytoma Diagnosis
What is the first diagnostic test for pheochromocytoma?
Answer
Plasma free metanephrines or 24-hour urinary catecholamines/metanephrines (best sensitivity). Imaging (CT/MRI) only if biochemistry positive. Localization ≠ diagnosis.
Pheo: α-blocker First
Why must α-blockers be given BEFORE β-blockers in pheochromocytoma treatment?
Answer
β-blocker first → unopposed α-adrenergic effects → hypertensive crisis. α-blockers (phenoxybenzamine or phentolamine) block catecholamine effects first, THEN add β-blocker to prevent reflex tachycardia.
MEN Syndromes
What are the classic components of MEN2A vs MEN2B?
Answer
MEN2A (RET): Medullary thyroid cancer + Pheochromocytoma + Hyperparathyroidism. MEN2B (RET): MTC + Pheo + Mucosal neuromas + Marfanoid habitus (NO hyperparathyroidism).
Primary vs Secondary AI
What is the key lab differentiator between primary and secondary adrenal insufficiency?
Answer
Primary: ↑ACTH (pituitary tries to compensate), hyperpigmentation, both glucocorticoid AND mineralocorticoid deficiency. Secondary: ↓ACTH, no hyperpigmentation, glucocorticoid deficiency only (aldosterone preserved via RAS).
Zona Glomerulosa Limitation
Why can the zona glomerulosa NOT make cortisol even with maximal ACTH stimulation?
Answer
Lacks 17α-hydroxylase → cannot convert pregnenolone to 17-OH pregnenolone → blocks cortisol synthesis. Structurally limited to mineralocorticoid synthesis (GFR mnemonic).
Multiple Choice Practice
Question 1
A 35-year-old male presents with episodic severe hypertension (BP 180/120), diaphoresis, severe headache, and palpitations. CT shows a 4 cm right adrenal mass. Which drug should be given FIRST before surgical resection?
A Metoprolol
B Esmolol infusion
C Phenoxybenzamine
D Nitroprusside
E Hydralazine
Answer: C (Phenoxybenzamine)
Why: Pheochromocytoma → catecholamine excess. Must block α-adrenergic effects FIRST with phenoxybenzamine (long-acting) or phentolamine (short-acting). β-blockade must come SECOND to prevent unopposed α effects and hypertensive crisis. Other agents are not first-line for pheo prep.
Question 2
Which MEN syndrome includes hyperparathyroidism as a component?
A MEN1
B MEN2A
C MEN2B
D MEN2A and MEN2B
E All three
Answer: B and A (both correct)
Why: MEN1 = "3 Ps" (Parathyroid, Pituitary, Pancreas). MEN2A = Medullary thyroid + Pheo + Hyperparathyroidism. MEN2B = Medullary thyroid + Pheo + Mucosal neuromas (NO hyperparathyroidism). Both MEN1 and MEN2A have hyperparathyroidism; MEN2B does NOT.
Question 3
A 16-year-old with severe sepsis from meningococcemia develops purpuric rash, DIC, and acute cardiovascular collapse. Autopsy reveals bilateral adrenal hemorrhage with necrosis. Which syndrome describes this presentation?
A Cushing's syndrome
B Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome
C Nelson's syndrome
D Conn's syndrome
E Addisonian crisis (chronic)
Answer: B (Waterhouse-Friderichsen)
Why: Neisseria meningitidis sepsis → bilateral adrenal hemorrhage → acute adrenal insufficiency → shock. Classic presentation: meningococcemia + purpura + adrenal necrosis. Acute, life-threatening adrenal crisis.
Question 4
A woman presents with hypertension, hypokalemia, virilization (facial hair, clitoromegaly), and elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone. Another patient has hypertension, hypokalemia, virilization but NORMAL 17-OHP. How do you distinguish these two enzyme deficiencies?
A 17-OHP level (first has 21-OH; second has 11β-OH deficiency)
B Urinary cortisol (first has more severe deficiency)
C Blood pressure response to treatment
D Ultrasound of adrenals
E ACTH level
Answer: A (17-OHP)
Why: 21-OH deficiency: ↑17-OHP (pregnenolone backs up before 21-hydroxylase step). 11β-OH deficiency: Normal 17-OHP but ↑11-deoxycortisol + ↑DOC (upstream accumulation after 17α-OH step). 17-OHP is the marker enzyme deficiency differentiator.
Question 5
Why is the zona glomerulosa unable to synthesize cortisol regardless of ACTH level?
A Lacks StAR protein
B Lacks 17α-hydroxylase (CYP17A1)
C Lacks 11β-hydroxylase
D Lacks 21-hydroxylase
E Cannot respond to ACTH
Answer: B (Lacks 17α-hydroxylase)
Why: 17α-hydroxylase is the gateway enzyme converting pregnenolone to 17-OH pregnenolone (essential for cortisol branch). Zona glomerulosa LACKS this enzyme → can ONLY make mineralocorticoids. GFR mnemonic enforces this limitation.
Question 6
A patient with hypertension and hypokalemia has low plasma renin activity and high aldosterone concentration. CT shows a unilateral adrenal nodule. What is the next diagnostic step?
A Start spironolactone
B Adrenal vein sampling
C Immediate surgery
D Repeat aldosterone:renin ratio
E PET/CT scan
Answer: B (Adrenal vein sampling)
Why: Biochemistry confirms Conn's (↑aldosterone, ↓renin). Imaging (CT) shows adenoma location. Adrenal vein sampling confirms SOURCE (unilateral vs bilateral). If unilateral → surgical candidate. If bilateral hyperplasia → medical management (spironolactone/eplerenone).
Question 7
A patient with Cushing's disease undergoes bilateral adrenalectomy. Years later, develops worsening hyperpigmentation, visual field defects, and enlarging pituitary tumor. What is the diagnosis?
A Recurrent Cushing's disease
B Nelson's syndrome
C Ectopic ACTH syndrome
D Secondary Addison's disease
E Adrenal rest carcinoma
Answer: B (Nelson's Syndrome)
Why: Post-bilateral adrenalectomy → loss of cortisol feedback inhibition → ACTH-secreting pituitary tumor grows unopposed → ↑ACTH → hyperpigmentation + pituitary mass effects (visual field defects, CN compression). Prevention: pituitary radiation or medical therapy after adrenalectomy.
Question 8
A patient presents with weakness, weight loss, hyperpigmentation, hyponatremia (Na 128), and hyperkalemia (K 6.2). What distinguishes PRIMARY adrenal insufficiency from secondary AI?
A Cortisol level
B ACTH level and hyperpigmentation + hyponatremia + hyperkalemia
C Urinary free cortisol
D Response to ACTH stimulation test
E MRI findings
Answer: B (ACTH + clinical features)
Why: Primary AI = ↑ACTH (pituitary compensates) + hyperpigmentation (ACTH stimulates melanocytes) + hyponatremia + hyperkalemia (mineralocorticoid deficiency). Secondary AI = ↓ACTH (no stimulation) + no hyperpigmentation + hyponatremia only (aldosterone preserved via RAS).
Question 9
A 28-year-old woman presents with hirsutism, oligomenorrhea, and infertility. Labs show elevated LH:FSH ratio and elevated testosterone. 17-OHP is normal. DHEA-S is mildly elevated. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A Late-onset 21-OH CAH
B PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)
C Androgen-secreting tumor
D Ovarian hyperandrogenism
E Cushing's syndrome
Answer: B (PCOS)
Why: Both PCOS and late-onset CAH present with hirsutism, irregular periods, elevated androgens. Key differentiator: 17-OHP is NORMAL in PCOS but ↑↑ in CAH. DHEA-S is mildly elevated in both, but normal 17-OHP rules out CAH. Pelvic ultrasound would show cysts in PCOS.
Question 10
In a patient with suspected Cushing's syndrome, the 1mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test is abnormal (cortisol not suppressed). High-dose dexamethasone (8mg) is then given, and cortisol decreases by 60%. What is the most likely source of hypercortisolism?
A Primary adrenal adenoma/carcinoma
B Pituitary Cushing's disease
C Ectopic ACTH (small cell lung cancer)
D Ectopic CRH syndrome
E Non-ACTH secreting adrenal carcinoma
Answer: B (Pituitary Cushing's Disease)
Why: High-dose DST suppresses by ≥50% ONLY in pituitary Cushing's disease (feedback-sensitive ACTH tumor). Ectopic ACTH and adrenal sources do NOT suppress with high-dose DST (autonomous secretion). Pituitary accounts for 70% of Cushing's syndrome.
Question 11
A woman with hirsutism and irregular menses has elevated DHEA-S (700 ng/dL; normal <300). Which organ is the likely source of excess androgen?
A Ovary
B Adrenal gland
C Liver
D Pituitary
E Thyroid
Answer: B (Adrenal gland)
Why: DHEA-S is produced ONLY by adrenal zona reticularis. Ovarian tumors produce testosterone/androstenedione (not DHEA-S). Elevated DHEA-S with hirsutism → adrenal androgen excess (CAH, Cushing's, or adrenal tumor). Check 17-OHP to rule out CAH.
Question 12
A 40-year-old with pheochromocytoma has been started on metoprolol by another physician. Hours later, develops severe hypertensive crisis (BP 220/140) and refusal to operate. Why did this happen?
A Metoprolol dose was too low
B Unopposed α-adrenergic effects from catecholamine surge
C Patient developed anaphylaxis
D Tumor necrosis released catecholamines
E Metoprolol is contraindicated in pheo
Answer: B (Unopposed α effects)
Why: β-blockers without prior α-blockade allow catecholamine α-effects to predominate unopposed → severe HTN. MUST give α-blocker FIRST (phenoxybenzamine days to weeks), then add β-blocker to prevent reflex tachycardia. This is a critical sequencing rule in pheo management.
Step 1-Style Case Presentations
VIGNETTE 1 — Neonatal Presentation
A 3-day-old 46,XX infant presents with poor feeding, lethargy, hypotension, hyponatremia (Na 125), and hyperkalemia (K 7.1). Vital signs show shock requiring ICU admission. On examination, the infant has virilization with clitoromegaly and labial fusion. Labs reveal elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP 18 ng/mL; normal <3). The mother reports no prior endocrine history.
Diagnosis: 21-Hydroxylase Deficiency (Salt-Wasting Form)
Classic neonatal presentation with BOTH virilization AND salt-wasting crisis. Elevated 17-OHP is diagnostic. 46,XX virilization from excess androgens. Treatment: hydrocortisone + fludrocortisone + IV fluids/electrolyte correction.
VIGNETTE 2 — Adolescent Hypertension
A 16-year-old female presents for evaluation of hypertension (BP 155/95) discovered on school screening. She complains of hirsutism, irregular menstrual periods, and clitoral enlargement over the past 2 years. Labs show elevated 17-OHP (8.2 ng/mL), normal renin (1.5 ng/mL/hr), and mild hypokalemia (K 3.4). Cortisol is normal. 24h UFC is normal. No spontaneous hypokalemia.
Diagnosis: 11β-Hydroxylase Deficiency
Key clue: HTN + virilization + elevated 17-OHP. Unlike 21-OH, aldosterone is preserved (renin normal) but DOC accumulates → HTN + hypokalemia. Distinguish from Conn's by elevated 17-OHP and adrenal source. Treatment: dexamethasone to suppress ACTH.
VIGNETTE 3 — Primary Amenorrhea + HTN
A 18-year-old 46,XY individual with female external genitalia presents with primary amenorrhea and hypertension (BP 158/98). Labs reveal cortisol very low (3 μg/dL), ACTH high (118 pg/mL), elevated 11-deoxycortisol (8.5 ng/dL), and no uterus on ultrasound. 17-OHP is normal. Karyotype confirms 46,XY.
Diagnosis: 17α-Hydroxylase Deficiency
46,XY with phenotypic female (no testosterone → Wolffian ducts regress; no DHT → female external genitalia). Primary amenorrhea (no estrogen/androgen for endometrial proliferation). HTN + hypokalemia from mineralocorticoid excess. Treatment: hydrocortisone (suppresses excess DOC + restores glucocorticoid).
VIGNETTE 4 — Episodic Hypertension + Sweating
A 35-year-old male presents to the ED with acute severe headache, diaphoresis, and BP 185/110. Wife notes he has had 3-4 episodes over the past month, each lasting 30-60 minutes, with palpitations and chest pressure. Between episodes, BP is 145/90. Physical exam shows no orthostatic changes. Plasma free metanephrines are elevated (650 pg/mL; normal <90). CT abdomen shows a 3.5 cm right adrenal mass.
Diagnosis: Pheochromocytoma (Right Adrenal)
Classic triad: episodic severe HTN, diaphoresis, headache. Elevated metanephrines confirm catecholamine excess. Next: α-blockade FIRST (phenoxybenzamine), then β-blocker, then surgical resection. Check for MEN2A/2B (pheochromocytoma in 50% of MEN2).
VIGNETTE 5 — Bilateral Pheo + Hypercalcemia
A 22-year-old male presents with hypertension and episodic sweating. Imaging shows bilateral adrenal masses. Plasma metanephrines are markedly elevated. Labs also show ionized calcium 5.8 mg/dL (high-normal), PTH 85 pg/mL (elevated). Thyroid ultrasound shows a 1.5 cm nodule with suspicious features. Genetic testing ordered.
Diagnosis: MEN2A (RET Mutation)
Bilateral pheochromocytoma + hyperparathyroidism + medullary thyroid cancer (thyroid nodule in young patient with MEN). 50% of MEN2A have bilateral pheo; always assess for other components. Treatment: prophylactic thyroidectomy, parathyroid surgery, pheo resection, RET screening of family.
VIGNETTE 6 — Meningococcemia + Shock
A 14-year-old boy admitted with severe meningococcal meningitis develops rapidly progressive shock, DIC with purpuric rash, and refractory hypotension despite fluid resuscitation and pressors. Cortisol at admission was 15 μg/dL (low-normal), but 4 hours later is 8 μg/dL. Imaging shows bilateral adrenal hemorrhage with necrosis. Patient dies from multiorgan failure within 24 hours.
Diagnosis: Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome
Neisseria meningitidis sepsis → bilateral adrenal hemorrhage → acute fulminant adrenal insufficiency → shock. Medical emergency. Treatment: immediate IV hydrocortisone + empiric antibiotics + aggressive support. Mortality high despite therapy. Prevention: meningococcal vaccination.
VIGNETTE 7 — Post-Adrenalectomy Pituitary Enlargement
A 52-year-old woman treated 2 years ago with bilateral adrenalectomy for severe Cushing's disease now returns with worsening symptoms. She reports progressive darkening of skin, bitemporal visual field defects, and headaches. MRI shows a 2.5 cm pituitary mass compressing the optic chiasm. ACTH is 412 pg/mL (markedly elevated). Cortisol is very low (adrenally insufficient).
Diagnosis: Nelson's Syndrome
Post-bilateral adrenalectomy: loss of cortisol feedback → ACTH-secreting adenoma grows unopposed → ↑ACTH → hyperpigmentation. Pituitary tumor mass effects (bitemporal hemianopia, headache). Should have received pituitary radiation or medical therapy post-adrenalectomy. Treatment: pituitary surgery/radiation, glucocorticoid + mineralocorticoid replacement.
VIGNETTE 8 — Ectopic ACTH + Rapid Onset
A 58-year-old male smoker develops severe hypokalemia (K 2.8), hypertension, hyperglycemia (glucose 285), and purple striae over 4 weeks. 24h UFC is 850 μg (very high). ACTH is 185 pg/mL. Low-dose DST shows no suppression. High-dose DST (8mg) shows no suppression (cortisol drops only 10%). CXH reveals a 3 cm hilar mass. CT chest confirms small cell lung cancer.
Diagnosis: Ectopic ACTH Syndrome (Small Cell Lung Cancer)
Rapid onset severe Cushing's (weeks), very high cortisol/ACTH, NO suppression on high-dose DST, hypokalemia out of proportion. Lung mass on imaging. Ectopic ACTH from malignancy is autonomous → resistant to dexamethasone feedback. Treatment: treat underlying cancer, metyrapone/mitotane for symptom control.
VIGNETTE 9 — Addison's Disease Workup
A 45-year-old woman presents with 3-month history of progressive fatigue, weight loss (10 lbs), hypotension (BP 95/58), and diffuse hyperpigmentation of skin and oral mucosa. Labs show hyponatremia (Na 128), hyperkalemia (K 6.5), cortisol 6 μg/dL (low), ACTH 156 pg/mL (high). Chest X-ray is clear. TB testing is negative.
Diagnosis: Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease) — Autoimmune
Classic presentation: ↓cortisol + ↑ACTH + hyperpigmentation + hyponatremia + hyperkalemia + hypotension. TB negative and clear CXR rule out tuberculous AI. Most likely: autoimmune (most common in US). Worldwide, TB is most common. Treatment: lifelong glucocorticoid + mineralocorticoid replacement.
VIGNETTE 10 — PCOS vs CAH Differentiation
A 24-year-old woman with irregular menstrual cycles, hirsutism, and obesity (BMI 32) has elevated testosterone (95 ng/dL; normal <70). LH:FSH ratio is elevated at 4.2. Pelvic ultrasound shows multiple small ovarian cysts. DHEA-S is 220 ng/dL (mildly elevated). Fasting glucose is 105 mg/dL (impaired fasting glucose). 17-OHP level is normal (0.8 ng/mL).
Diagnosis: PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)
Clinical features (hirsutism, amenorrhea, obesity) + ovarian cysts + elevated androgens + insulin resistance. KEY DIFFERENTIATOR: Normal 17-OHP rules out late-onset 21-OH CAH (which would have ↑17-OHP). PCOS does not require glucocorticoid therapy; CAH does. Management: oral contraceptives, metformin, lifestyle modification.

Pheochromocytoma Catecholamine-secreting adrenal tumor (medulla); episodic HTN, diaphoresis, headache; Rule of 10s (bilateral, malignant, extra-adrenal, pediatric); diagnosis: plasma metanephrines or 24h urinary catecholamines Episodic symptoms, severe HTN, MEN2A/2B association, RET mutation screening, α-blockade FIRST then β-blockade Must give α-blocker BEFORE β-blocker to prevent unopposed α-adrenergic crisis; biochemistry confirms diagnosis, imaging localizes
MEN Syndromes MEN1 (3Ps: Parathyroid, Pituitary, Pancreas); MEN2A (Medullary thyroid + Pheo + Hyperparathyroidism, RET mutation); MEN2B (MTC + Pheo + Mucosal neuromas + Marfanoid, RET mutation, NO hyperparathyroidism) RET proto-oncogene, bilateral pheo (50% in MEN2), medullary thyroid cancer screening, prophylactic thyroidectomy MEN2B has neuromas & marfanoid habitus (NO hyperparathyroidism); MEN2A has hyperparathyroidism; MEN1 has pituitary adenoma
