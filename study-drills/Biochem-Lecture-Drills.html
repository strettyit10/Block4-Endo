Vignette Identification Drill

Read stem → identify mechanism → reveal
Vignette 1-A
A researcher blocks adenylyl cyclase in thyroid follicular cells. She is studying which pituitary hormone's downstream signaling cascade.
→ Which hormone? Which G-protein? What second messenger is lost?
✅ Answer
TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone) via Gs protein → ↑adenylyl cyclase → ↑cAMP → PKA → thyroid hormone synthesis. Blocking adenylyl cyclase eliminates cAMP signaling.
Why it matters: TSH is a peptide hormone using Gs-cAMP pathway — classic Step 1 target for receptor coupling questions.
Vignette 1-B
A woman's uterine smooth muscle contracts when oxytocin is released. Intracellular calcium rises rapidly. A new drug blocks DAG-mediated signaling.
→ What G-protein is oxytocin using? What second messenger is DAG blocking?
✅ Answer
Gq protein → PLC → IP₃ + DAG. IP₃ releases Ca²⁺ from ER (drives contraction). DAG activates PKC. Drug blocking DAG reduces PKC activity but Ca²⁺ rise via IP₃ may persist.
Step 1 hook: Gq hormones = GnRH, TRH, Oxytocin, ADH (V1), AngII. Mnemonic: "GOTCHA" = Gq Ot TRH GnRH + ADH-V1 AngII
Vignette 1-C
Cortisol binds to a receptor in the cytoplasm. The hormone-receptor complex then translocates to the nucleus and binds to specific DNA sequences.
→ What type of receptor? What are those DNA sequences called? How long before response?
✅ Answer
Nuclear (intracellular) receptor. DNA sequences = Hormone Response Elements (HREs). Response time = hours to days (gene transcription must occur, then protein synthesis).
Contrast with membrane receptors: cAMP/IP₃ act in seconds. Nuclear receptors change gene expression — the response is slow but prolonged.
⚠️

Test Trap Drill

Classic confusers · Select the correct answer
🪤Trap 1-1 — The Catecholamine Trap
Epinephrine and norepinephrine are amines derived from tyrosine. Unlike thyroid hormones (also amines), they use:
✅ B is correct. Catecholamines are NOT lipophilic enough to enter cells despite being amines — they use surface GPCRs (α₁=Gq, α₂=Gi, β₁/β₂=Gs). ONLY thyroid hormone (T3/T4) uses nuclear receptors among amines. This is a classic Step 1 trap: "amine = surface receptor" EXCEPT T3/T4.
🪤Trap 1-2 — Somatostatin's Direction
Somatostatin is released from the hypothalamus AND from delta cells of the pancreas. Its effect on cAMP is:
✅ B is correct. Somatostatin uses Gi → ↓adenylyl cyclase → ↓cAMP. This INHIBITS GH, TSH, insulin, glucagon secretion. Gi proteins are inhibitory: somatostatin, dopamine (D2), α₂-adrenergic, opioids. Don't confuse with Gs (stimulatory).

Vignette Identification Drill

Read stem → identify → reveal
Vignette 2-A
A 3-day-old infant presents with poor feeding, vomiting, and ambiguous genitalia. Labs: Na⁺ 122, K⁺ 6.8, blood glucose 45. Serum 17-hydroxyprogesterone is markedly elevated.
→ Diagnosis? Enzyme deficiency? What accumulated? What is elevated on labs and why?
✅ Answer
Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH) — 21-hydroxylase deficiency. 17-OHP accumulates (cannot convert to DOC). ↓Aldosterone → salt-wasting (↓Na⁺, ↑K⁺). ↓Cortisol → ↑ACTH. Excess precursors shunted → androgens → ambiguous genitalia in XX.
Key identifier: 17-OHP elevation = 21-hydroxylase deficiency. Salt-wasting + virilization is the classic presentation.
Vignette 2-B
A 16-year-old female presents with hypertension, acne, and clitoromegaly. Labs: ↑androgens, ↓cortisol, normal-to-high aldosterone, ↑11-deoxycorticosterone. No salt-wasting.
→ Which CAH? Why hypertension? What distinguishes this from 21-hydroxylase deficiency?
✅ Answer
11β-hydroxylase deficiency. 11-deoxycorticosterone (DOC) accumulates and acts as a mineralocorticoid → Na⁺ retention → HTN. Androgens also accumulate → virilization. Key difference from 21-OH: HYPERTENSION not salt-wasting.
Mnemonic: 11β-OH = HTN (11 = "elevens" = elevated BP). Both 21 and 11β cause virilization; only 11β causes HTN.
Vignette 2-C
A 17-year-old XY individual presents as phenotypically female with primary amenorrhea, no pubic hair, and hypertension. Labs show ↑aldosterone, ↓cortisol, ↓androgens. Karyotype: 46,XY.
→ Enzyme deficiency? Why female phenotype in XY? Why no sex development in XX?
✅ Answer
17α-hydroxylase deficiency. Cannot make sex steroids (androgens OR estrogens) or cortisol. XY individuals: no androgens → no masculinization → female phenotype (but no uterus — testes present). XX individuals: no estrogens → no feminization. Both present with primary amenorrhea. ↑Aldosterone → HTN.
Distinguishing clue: NO sex development in either sex + HTN + ↑aldosterone. Always think 17α-OH when you see XY with female phenotype AND HTN.
⚠️

Test Trap Drill

Pick the right answer
🪤Trap 2-1 — Aldosterone Regulation
Which of the following directly regulates aldosterone secretion from the zona glomerulosa?
✅ B. Aldosterone is regulated by RAAS (Ang II) and ↑K⁺, NOT primarily by ACTH. ACTH has only minor/permissive effect. Low Na⁺ triggers RAAS (↑renin) indirectly but isn't the direct stimulus. This trap catches students who memorize "ACTH stimulates adrenal cortex" without distinguishing zone-specific regulation.
🪤Trap 2-2 — CAH BP Direction
A newborn with CAH has hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, and hypotension. This pattern is seen in:
✅ B. 21-hydroxylase — the only CAH causing SALT-WASTING (↓aldosterone → ↓Na⁺ reabsorption → hypotension, ↑K⁺). Both 11β and 17α cause HYPERTENSION (due to accumulating mineralocorticoid precursors). This is the most commonly tested CAH distinction.

5α-reductase: converts what to what? Where active?
tap to reveal
Testosterone → DHT (dihydrotestosterone)
Active in prostate, skin, external genitalia. DHT = more potent androgen; responsible for external genital virilization in fetal development
AIS: genotype, phenotype, gonads, hormone levels?

46,XY · Female phenotype · Testes (no uterus) · ↑Testosterone, ↑LH, ↑Estrogen
Androgen receptor defective → testosterone cannot act → female phenotype despite XY
5α-reductase deficiency: presentation at birth vs puberty?
tap to reveal
Birth: ambiguous/female external genitalia · Puberty: virilization occurs (testosterone surges)
Testosterone OK; DHT absent → no external genital virilization prenatally. At puberty, testosterone itself drives some virilization

tap to reveal
Sex Hormone Binding Globulin — binds testosterone and estradiol (bound = inactive)
↑Estrogen → ↑SHBG. ↑Insulin/obesity → ↓SHBG → more free androgens (PCOS mechanism)
In AIS: why is LH elevated?
tap to reveal
Androgen receptor defective → testosterone cannot negatively feed back on hypothalamus/pituitary → ↑LH
↑LH → ↑testosterone (but still can't act). High testosterone → aromatized → ↑estrogen → female secondary characteristics
AIS vs 5α-RD deficiency: key distinguishing feature?
tap to reveal
AIS: testosterone CANNOT act (receptor broken) → no virilization ever
5α-RD def: testosterone CAN act → virilization at puberty
Both: XY, female at birth, no uterus. AIS stays female; 5α-RD virilizes at puberty
Aromatase inhibitors (e.g., anastrozole) block what conversion?
tap to reveal
Block androgen → estrogen conversion (testosterone → estradiol; androstenedione → estrone)
Used in ER+ breast cancer; also inhibit peripheral estrogen in postmenopausal women

5α-RD: DHT broken → testosterone works → virilizes at puberty

Vignette Identification Drill

4 vignettes
Vignette 3-A
An 18-year-old with 46,XY karyotype presents with primary amenorrhea. She has normal breast development, sparse pubic/axillary hair, a blind-ending vagina, and no uterus on ultrasound. Testosterone level is in the normal male range. She has never had any virilization.
→ Diagnosis? Why breasts but no pubic hair? Why no virilization?
✅ Answer
Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS). Androgen receptor is non-functional → testosterone/DHT cannot act anywhere. Breasts develop because testosterone is aromatized to estrogen (estrogen acts normally). No pubic/axillary hair because hair follicle response requires androgen receptor. No uterus because testes produce MIF (Müllerian Inhibiting Factor) normally. No virilization ever because receptor is broken.
Classic Step 1 presentation: 46,XY + female phenotype + high testosterone + no pubic hair + no uterus = AIS.
Vignette 3-B
A child raised as female (46,XY) develops clitoral enlargement and voice deepening at puberty. As an infant, had ambiguous genitalia. Testosterone levels are normal male range; DHT is undetectable.
→ Diagnosis? What enzyme is deficient? Why did virilization occur at puberty?
✅ Answer
5α-reductase deficiency. Cannot convert testosterone → DHT. DHT required for external genitalia development prenatally → ambiguous/female external at birth. Androgen receptor is INTACT → at puberty, high testosterone levels (not DHT) can still drive virilization through the functioning receptor. Undetectable DHT is the diagnostic clue.
Key: T = normal, DHT = absent, AR = functional. Compare AIS: T = high, DHT available but receptor broken.
⚠️

Test Trap Drill

Classic confusers
🪤Trap 3-1 — Testosterone Levels in AIS vs 5α-RD
In both AIS and 5α-reductase deficiency, testosterone levels are in the male range. How do you differentiate them on labs?
✅ B. DHT is the distinguishing lab: absent in 5α-RD deficiency (can't make it), normal in AIS (can make DHT, just can't respond to it). Clinically, puberty-time virilization also distinguishes them.
🪤Trap 3-2 — SHBG and Free Hormone
A woman with obesity and insulin resistance has regular menstrual cycles but signs of androgen excess (hirsutism, acne). Total testosterone is borderline. Why might she still have androgen excess?
✅ B. Hyperinsulinemia suppresses hepatic SHBG production → ↓SHBG → more free testosterone → androgen excess despite "normal" total testosterone. This is the PCOS mechanism. Always think: what matters is FREE hormone, not total.

ADH (vasopressin) V1 receptor: where, G-protein, effect?
tap to reveal
V1: Vascular smooth muscle · Gq · Vasoconstriction
IP₃/DAG → ↑Ca²⁺ → smooth muscle contraction → ↑BP
ADH V2 receptor: where, G-protein, effect?
tap to reveal
V2: Collecting duct principal cells · Gs · ↑AQP-2 insertion → water reabsorption
cAMP → PKA → AQP-2 exocytosis to luminal membrane → antidiuresis
What triggers ADH release?
tap to reveal
↑Serum osmolality (main) · ↓Blood volume/pressure · Nausea · Drugs (opioids, carbamazepine)
Osmoreceptors in hypothalamus detect ↑osmolality (as low as 1%) and fire ADH
Central DI: cause, labs, treatment?
tap to reveal
↓ADH production (pituitary/hypothalamus damage) · ↑Serum osm, ↓urine osm, dilute polyuria · Rx: Desmopressin (DDAVP)
Responds to desmopressin → confirms central (has intact V2 receptors)
Nephrogenic DI: cause, labs, treatment?
tap to reveal
↓V2 receptor response (kidneys can't respond to ADH) · Same lab picture · Rx: Thiazides + low-Na diet + NSAIDs (indomethacin)
Does NOT respond to desmopressin — receptor is broken. Lithium and hypercalcemia are common causes
Gestational DI: mechanism, treatment?
tap to reveal
Placental vasopressinase destroys ADH · Rx: Desmopressin (resistant to vasopressinase)
Responds to DDAVP (unlike nephrogenic). Resolves after delivery
Water deprivation test → no response → give desmopressin → urine concentrates. Diagnosis?
tap to reveal
Central DI (or Gestational DI)
Desmopressin response = intact V2 receptor = problem is ADH production/supply
Oxytocin: 2 main effects?
tap to reveal
1. Uterine contraction (labor) · 2. Milk letdown (ejection from breast)
Uses Gq receptor. Synthesized in hypothalamus, stored in posterior pituitary
Why do thiazides treat nephrogenic DI (paradoxically)?
tap to reveal
Thiazides → ↓extracellular volume → ↑proximal tubule Na⁺/water reabsorption → less water reaches collecting duct → less dilute urine
Paradoxical: diuretic used to reduce urine output by shifting reabsorption upstream
🧠 DI Diagnosis Ladder: Desmopressin Response TestPolyuria + dilute urine + ↑serum Osm → Water deprivation → still dilute → Give DDAVP:
→ Urine concentrates = Central or Gestational DI
→ Urine stays dilute = Nephrogenic DI
→ No polyuria to begin with (concentrates with deprivation) = Primary polydipsia

Vignette Identification Drill

4 vignettes
Vignette 4-A
A 35-year-old man with a history of head trauma 2 months ago presents with extreme thirst, polyuria (10L/day), and nocturia. Serum Na⁺ = 152, serum osmolality = 305. Urine osmolality = 80 (very dilute). After water deprivation, urine remains dilute. After desmopressin injection, urine osmolality rises to 550.
→ Diagnosis? What is the mechanism? What does the desmopressin response tell you?
✅ Answer
Central Diabetes Insipidus. Head trauma damaged posterior pituitary/hypothalamus → ↓ADH production. Without ADH, V2 receptors unstimulated → no AQP-2 insertion → dilute polyuria. Desmopressin response: urine concentrates because V2 receptors are intact — they just had no ADH signal. This confirms central (not nephrogenic) DI.
Rule: desmopressin response = central DI. No response = nephrogenic DI.
Vignette 4-B
A 45-year-old man on lithium for bipolar disorder develops polyuria and polydipsia. Serum Na⁺ = 148. After water deprivation + desmopressin: urine osmolality rises only minimally (120 → 135).
→ Diagnosis? What is lithium doing to the kidney? What is the treatment?
✅ Answer
Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus secondary to lithium. Lithium blocks V2 receptor signaling (downregulates cAMP pathway) and reduces AQP-2 expression → collecting duct cannot reabsorb water even when ADH is present. No response to desmopressin confirms receptor problem. Treatment: amiloride (blocks lithium entry into cells), thiazides + low-Na diet, NSAIDs.
Lithium = classic cause of nephrogenic DI. Hypercalcemia is another. Always think "lithium → nephrogenic" on Step 1.
Vignette 4-C
A pregnant woman at 28 weeks develops polyuria, polydipsia, and elevated serum Na⁺. She responds to desmopressin but not to native ADH infusion.
→ Diagnosis? What is the mechanism unique to pregnancy?
✅ Answer
Gestational DI. Placenta produces vasopressinase, which rapidly degrades native ADH. Desmopressin is resistant to vasopressinase → therefore responds. Native ADH infusion → degraded → no response. Resolves after delivery. Managed with DDAVP.
Key clue: responds to DDAVP but NOT to native ADH = vasopressinase activity (gestational). This is rare but Step 1 loves rare in-pregnancy associations.
⚠️

Test Trap Drill

3 traps
🪤Trap 4-1 — Primary Polydipsia vs Central DI
A 30-year-old psychiatric patient drinks 8-10L water daily and urinates frequently. Serum Na⁺ = 136 (low-normal). Serum osmolality = 272. Urine osmolality = 85.
✅ C. In DI (any type), serum Na and Osm are HIGH (water loss without intake matching). In primary polydipsia: patient drinks so much that serum becomes dilute → LOW serum osmolality + LOW Na. Urine is dilute because ADH is appropriately suppressed by low Osm. This distinction saves you on Step 1.
🪤Trap 4-2 — V1 vs V2 Receptor Drug Targets
Desmopressin (DDAVP) is selective for which vasopressin receptor and therefore avoids which side effect?
✅ A. DDAVP is V2-selective → promotes water reabsorption without vasoconstriction. Used for DI, nocturnal enuresis, and hemophilia A (V2 also triggers vWF release). Native ADH acts on both V1 (vasoconstriction) and V2 (antidiuresis).

Lecture 5: Thyroid Hormone Synthesis & Action

Iodide trapping · TPO steps · T3/T4 · Inhibitors · Graves vs Hashimoto

Biochem · Lecture 5 of 5
🃏

Flashcard Flip Drill

Click to flip • 10 cards
Steps of thyroid hormone synthesis in order?
tap to reveal
1.Iodide trapping (NIS) → 2.Oxidation (TPO) → 3.Organification (TPO) → 4.Coupling (TPO) → 5.Storage as Tg → 6.Proteolysis → 7.Release T3/T4
Mnemonic: "I Owe Oregon Couples Some Perfect Release"
What is organification?
tap to reveal
Iodide (I⁻) oxidized to I₂ then attached to tyrosine residues on thyroglobulin → forms MIT (monoiodotyrosine) and DIT (diiodotyrosine)
TPO catalyzes both oxidation and organification. PTU and methimazole both block organification.
Coupling reaction: what produces T3 and T4?
tap to reveal
DIT + DIT → T4 (thyroxine) | MIT + DIT → T3 (triiodothyronine)
TPO also catalyzes coupling. T4 is prohormone; T3 is the active form.
T4 → T3 conversion: where, enzyme?
tap to reveal
Peripheral tissues (liver, kidney, muscle) · 5'-deiodinase removes one iodine from outer ring
PTU (but NOT methimazole) inhibits peripheral T4→T3 conversion — important in thyroid storm
PTU vs Methimazole: what does each block?
tap to reveal
PTU: blocks TPO (organification/coupling) + blocks T4→T3 conversion
Methimazole: blocks TPO ONLY
PTU preferred in 1st trimester and thyroid storm. Methimazole preferred otherwise (longer half-life, once daily).
Graves disease: autoantibody? Effect on TSH?
tap to reveal
TSI (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin) activates TSH receptor → ↑T3/T4 → suppresses TSH
Classic triad: hyperthyroidism + exophthalmos + pretibial myxedema. TSH = very low (suppressed)
Hashimoto thyroiditis: antibodies + progression?
tap to reveal
Anti-TPO + Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies. Initially may have transient hyperthyroid (hashitoxicosis) → then hypothyroid
Most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient areas. Lymphocytic infiltration + Hürthle cells
Wolff-Chaikoff effect vs Jod-Basedow?
tap to reveal
Wolff-Chaikoff: excess iodide INHIBITS thyroid synthesis (transient, used for thyroid storm prep)
Jod-Basedow: excess iodide TRIGGERS hyperthyroidism in susceptible glands
Both involve excess iodide — opposite effects depending on context. HY for Step 1.
T3 nuclear receptor action: effect on metabolism?
tap to reveal
T3 binds nuclear receptor → ↑gene transcription → ↑basal metabolic rate, ↑O₂ consumption, ↑thermogenesis
Also: ↑β-adrenergic receptors → ↑HR, ↑cardiac output; ↑bone turnover; CNS maturation in fetus
Radioactive iodine (RAI) mechanism + use?
tap to reveal
¹³¹I trapped by NIS → concentrated in thyroid → β-radiation destroys follicular cells
Used for Graves, toxic nodular goiter, thyroid cancer. Contraindicated in pregnancy. May worsen Graves ophthalmopathy.
🧠 Thyroid Synthesis Steps — Mnemonic: "NIS-TPO-Tg-Out"NIS traps iodide → TPO oxidizes + organifies + couples → stored in Thyroglobulin → Proteolysis releases T3/T4 out. PTU blocks TPO + T4→T3. Methimazole blocks TPO only.

Vignette Identification Drill

4 vignettes
Vignette 5-A
A 28-year-old woman presents with palpitations, heat intolerance, weight loss despite increased appetite, and exophthalmos. TSH is undetectable. Free T4 is markedly elevated. Thyroid is diffusely enlarged and warm.
→ Diagnosis? Autoantibody? Why is TSH low?
✅ Answer
Graves disease. TSI (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin) binds and chronically activates the TSH receptor → autonomously produces T3/T4 → TSH suppressed by negative feedback (very low/undetectable TSH). Diffuse goiter (uniform stimulation), exophthalmos, pretibial myxedema are the classic triad.
TSH is suppressed (not absent due to destruction like Hashimoto hashitoxicosis phase). Anti-TSH receptor antibodies = pathognomonic.
Vignette 5-B
A patient in thyroid storm is being managed. You need to give PTU. A medical student asks: "Can't we just use methimazole — it works faster?"
→ Why is PTU preferred specifically in thyroid storm? What does PTU do that methimazole doesn't?
✅ Answer
PTU blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion (via inhibiting 5'-deiodinase), in addition to blocking TPO. In thyroid storm, rapid reduction of the active hormone T3 is critical. Methimazole only blocks new synthesis (TPO). Since existing T4 stores continue converting to T3, PTU's dual action is critical for acute crises.
Step 1 favorite: PTU = preferred in thyroid storm AND 1st trimester (methimazole teratogenic). Otherwise, methimazole preferred.
Vignette 5-C
A 42-year-old woman has fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, hair loss, and bradycardia. TSH is elevated. Free T4 is low. Labs show positive anti-TPO and anti-thyroglobulin antibodies. Thyroid biopsy shows lymphocytic infiltration.
→ Diagnosis? What is "hashitoxicosis"? What is the biopsy finding on histology?
✅ Answer
Hashimoto thyroiditis (chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis). Autoimmune destruction of thyroid → hypothyroid. Hashitoxicosis: early in disease, follicle destruction releases stored T3/T4 → transient hyperthyroid phase before hypothyroidism. Histology: lymphocytic infiltration + germinal centers + Hürthle cell change (oxyphilic metaplasia).
Most common cause of hypothyroidism in US. Anti-TPO is the most sensitive marker. ↑TSH = primary hypothyroid (pituitary trying to stimulate failing gland).
⚠️

Test Trap Drill

3 traps
🪤Trap 5-1 — Wolff-Chaikoff vs Jod-Basedow
A patient with a multinodular goiter receives IV contrast for a CT scan. Two weeks later she develops thyrotoxicosis. This is:
✅ B. Jod-Basedow. In autonomous nodular tissue, iodide flood from contrast allows unrestricted hormone synthesis → thyrotoxicosis. Wolff-Chaikoff is the opposite: a normal gland temporarily shuts down synthesis when flooded with iodide. The key distinction: autonomous (nodular) tissue → Jod-Basedow; normal gland → Wolff-Chaikoff.
🪤Trap 5-2 — Methimazole in Pregnancy
A 10-week pregnant woman with Graves disease needs antithyroid drug therapy. What should you prescribe?
✅ B. PTU in 1st trimester. Methimazole associated with aplasia cutis and choanal atresia (teratogenic in 1st trimester). PTU preferred then. After 1st trimester, switch to methimazole (PTU has rare risk of hepatotoxicity). RAI is absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy.

Integration A — Lectures 2 + 3
A 2-week-old infant (46,XX) presents with salt-wasting crisis. On exam: clitoromegaly, labial fusion, hyperpigmented skin. Lab: 17-OHP very high; testosterone elevated; ACTH elevated; aldosterone low; cortisol low. Genetic testing confirms 21-hydroxylase mutation.
→ Explain the full pathophysiology: why each abnormal lab, and why virilization in an XX individual.
✅ Full Integration Answer
21-hydroxylase blocked: Cannot convert 17-OHP → 11-deoxycorticosterone (DOC) or 17-OHP → 11-deoxycortisol.

↓Cortisol: Loss of negative feedback → ↑ACTH → adrenal hyperplasia → drives all upstream precursors → excess 17-OHP spills into androgen pathway → ↑testosterone + DHEA.

↓Aldosterone: DOC also not made → no mineralocorticoid → ↑Na⁺ loss + ↑K⁺ → salt-wasting crisis.

Virilization in XX: Excess androgens cross-react with androgen receptor in female fetus → clitoromegaly, labial fusion.

Hyperpigmentation: ↑ACTH also stimulates melanocortin receptors (MSH-like activity) → skin darkening.
This integrates L2 (adrenal zones, CAH, steroid pathway) with L3 (sex steroids, androgen receptor). Full synthesis question.
Integration B — Lectures 1 + 4
A patient's V2 receptors are stimulated by desmopressin. You block the Gs protein with pertussis toxin analogue. Predict: will urine concentrate?
→ Explain why or why not based on receptor signaling.
✅ Answer
No — urine will NOT concentrate. V2 receptor uses Gs → adenylyl cyclase → ↑cAMP → PKA → AQP-2 insertion into luminal membrane. Blocking Gs with a pertussis toxin-like agent disrupts cAMP generation → PKA not activated → AQP-2 not inserted → water cannot be reabsorbed → dilute urine persists despite desmopressin binding.
Integrates L1 (GPCR signaling, Gs=cAMP) with L4 (V2 receptor mechanism). Step 1 style pharmacology question.
Integration C — Lectures 3 + 5
A woman with Graves disease is treated with PTU. After 3 months, her free T4 normalizes but she still has tachycardia and tremor. Her TSH remains suppressed.
→ Why is TSH still suppressed? What other drug can target her ongoing symptoms?
✅ Answer
TSH still suppressed because TSI (stimulating antibodies) persist even after T4 normalizes. The pituitary's thyrotrophs are adjusted to long-term suppression — TSH recovery lags behind T4 normalization by weeks to months (thyrotroph "sluggishness"). Ongoing tachycardia/tremor = adrenergic symptoms due to ↑β-receptor upregulation from chronic hyperthyroid state → add propranolol (β-blocker) to control symptoms without affecting thyroid synthesis.
Integrates L1 (receptor upregulation), L5 (Graves autoantibodies, PTU, β-blocker use for symptomatic control).
Integration D — Lectures 2 + 4
A patient with nephrogenic DI is started on hydrochlorothiazide. Explain: (1) why a diuretic reduces urine output in DI, and (2) why the same drug would be inappropriate for central DI.
→ Full mechanism required.
✅ Answer
(1) Thiazide in nephrogenic DI: Thiazide blocks NCC in distal tubule → ↓Na⁺ reabsorption → mild volume depletion → compensatory ↑proximal tubule reabsorption (Na⁺ + water) → less water delivered to collecting duct → less polyuria. Paradoxical — works because it bypasses the broken V2/AQP-2 system.

(2) Central DI: Treat with DDAVP (desmopressin) directly — V2 receptors are intact; just supply the missing ADH analog. Thiazide is unnecessary and less effective than replacing the missing hormone directly.
Integrates L4 (DI types, mechanism) with diuretic pharmacology. Tests depth of understanding, not memorization.
