Vignette 1Lectures 1 + 2 — GPCR signaling + CAH
A researcher blocks Gs protein activity in adrenocortical cells and then administers ACTH. She observes no cortisol production. When she repeats the experiment with a different blocker targeting Gq proteins instead, ACTH still fails to drive cortisol synthesis.
→ Explain: (1) which G-protein ACTH normally uses, (2) why the Gq blocker had the same result, (3) what downstream pathway is disrupted.
✅ Full Mechanistic Answer
ACTH → MC2R (melanocortin receptor) → Gs → ↑adenylyl cyclase → ↑cAMP → PKA → phosphorylates StAR protein → cholesterol enters mitochondria → CYP11A1 → pregnenolone → cortisol pathway
(1) ACTH uses Gs (not Gq). Blocking Gs eliminates cAMP → no StAR protein phosphorylation → cholesterol cannot enter mitochondria → no cortisol.

(2) Gq blocker has the same result because ACTH does NOT use Gq. Cortisol production is 100% Gs-dependent in the fasciculata. The Gq blocker is simply irrelevant — it blocks something that was never engaged.

(3) Disrupted pathway: Gs → cAMP → PKA → StAR → cholesterol import. Without StAR, steroidogenesis cannot begin regardless of substrate availability.
Combines L1 (GPCR Gs vs Gq) with L2 (ACTH → cortisol pathway). Classic Step 1 style: "researcher blocks X, what happens?"
Vignette 2Lectures 2 + 3 — CAH + Sex Steroids
A 3-day-old 46,XX infant presents with ambiguous genitalia, hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, and hypotension. Serum 17-OHP is markedly elevated. The pediatric endocrinologist explains that the enzyme deficiency has redirected steroidogenesis toward sex steroid production.
→ Draw the full pathway explaining: what enzyme is deficient, what accumulates, where it goes, and why both XX and XY infants are affected differently.
✅ Full Answer
Cholesterol → Pregnenolone → Progesterone → 17-OHP ──[21-OH blocked]──→ Cannot → DOC/Cortisol/Aldosterone
Instead: 17-OHP → 17,20-lyase → DHEA → Androstenedione → Testosterone → (aromatase) → Estradiol
21-hydroxylase deficiency: 17-OHP accumulates. Without the ability to make DOC → ↓aldosterone → salt-wasting. ↓Cortisol → ↑ACTH → drives all upstream precursors harder → excess spills into androgen pathway.

XX infants: Excess androgens act on androgen receptor → virilization → ambiguous genitalia (clitoromegaly, labial fusion). Gonads = normal ovaries.

XY infants: Already have high androgens normally. CAH may not cause obvious genital ambiguity but does cause salt-wasting crisis. Later: precocious puberty, early closure of growth plates.
Integrates L2 (21-OH deficiency, CAH pathophysiology) with L3 (androgen receptor action, sex steroid pathway). The most tested CAH vignette on Step 1.
Vignette 3Lectures 3 + 4 — Sex Steroids + ADH signaling
A pregnant woman at 32 weeks has polyuria. Her obstetrician notes that placental vasopressinase is degrading endogenous ADH. She is given native ADH infusion — no effect. She is then given desmopressin (DDAVP) — urine concentrates normally.
→ Why did DDAVP work when native ADH didn't? What receptor does DDAVP target, and through what G-protein? Why is this relevant to the sex steroid context of pregnancy?
✅ Answer
Why DDAVP worked: DDAVP is a synthetic ADH analog resistant to vasopressinase (structural modification). Native ADH is rapidly degraded. Both target the same V2 receptor.

V2 receptor pathway: V2 → Gs → ↑cAMP → PKA → AQP-2 exocytosis to luminal membrane → water reabsorption in collecting duct.

Sex steroid relevance: Estrogen (high in pregnancy) upregulates vasopressinase expression in the placenta — this is why gestational DI is a pregnancy-specific condition. Estrogen also resets the osmostat lower in pregnancy (tolerate lower serum osmolality). These combined effects make pregnant women uniquely susceptible to water dysregulation.
Integrates L3 (estrogen, pregnancy) with L4 (gestational DI, V2/Gs/cAMP, DDAVP vs native ADH). Tests depth: not just "DDAVP for gestational DI" but WHY.
Vignette 4Lectures 4 + 5 — ADH + Thyroid
A 55-year-old woman with known hypothyroidism (not on treatment) presents with confusion and serum Na⁺ of 122 (hyponatremia). Urine osmolality is elevated (600). Serum osmolality is low (255). TSH is markedly elevated.
→ What is causing the hyponatremia? How does hypothyroidism lead to inappropriate ADH secretion? What is the diagnosis?
✅ Answer
Diagnosis: SIADH secondary to hypothyroidism.

Hypothyroidism → ↓cardiac output → ↓effective arterial blood volume → baroreceptors perceive low volume → trigger non-osmotic ADH release → water retention → dilutional hyponatremia despite low serum osmolality (concentrated urine because ADH is active).

Labs fit SIADH: low serum Na⁺, low serum Osm, inappropriately concentrated urine (Uosm > serum Osm). High TSH confirms primary hypothyroidism as the cause.

Treatment: correct hypothyroidism (levothyroxine) + water restriction. Avoid rapid sodium correction (risk of osmotic demyelination syndrome).
Integrates L4 (ADH regulation, non-osmotic release) with L5 (Hashimoto/hypothyroidism → ↑TSH). SIADH from hypothyroidism is Step 1 HY.
Vignette 5Lectures 1 + 5 — Nuclear Receptors + Thyroid Action
T3 binds its receptor in the nucleus and alters gene transcription. A genetic mutation eliminates the DNA-binding domain of the thyroid hormone receptor. The patient has high T3/T4, high TSH, and no clinical hyperthyroidism.
→ Explain the mechanism of thyroid hormone resistance. Why is TSH elevated despite high T3/T4?
✅ Answer
Thyroid hormone resistance (Refetoff syndrome). The mutated thyroid receptor cannot bind HRE (hormone response elements in DNA) → T3 cannot alter gene transcription → no metabolic effects despite high hormone levels.

Pituitary thyrotrophs are also resistant → cannot detect T3/T4 → no negative feedback → ↑TRH → ↑TSH → ↑T3/T4 (a futile loop).

Clinically: patient appears euthyroid or mildly symptomatic because peripheral tissues also resist. Unlike central hyperthyroidism (TSH-secreting adenoma), this is receptor-level resistance in all tissues.

Key Step 1 flag: ↑TSH + ↑T3/T4 + euthyroid = think thyroid hormone resistance (vs. TSH-oma = similar labs but hyperthyroid symptoms).
Integrates L1 (nuclear receptor, HRE, gene transcription) with L5 (T3/T4 feedback, TSH regulation). Tests whether you understand the receptor mechanism, not just the hormone levels.
Vignette 6Lectures 2 + 5 — Adrenal + Thyroid Autoimmune
A 38-year-old woman with known Hashimoto thyroiditis presents with severe fatigue, hyperpigmentation, and hypotension. Her cortisol is low, ACTH is very high. TSH is elevated, free T4 is low. She is HLA-DR3/DR4 positive.
→ What is the additional diagnosis? How do both conditions share a pathophysiologic mechanism? What is the triad called?
✅ Answer
Addison disease (primary adrenal insufficiency) + Hashimoto thyroiditis = Schmidt syndrome (autoimmune polyglandular syndrome type 2 / APS-2).

Shared mechanism: Both are T-cell mediated autoimmune destruction of endocrine glands. Autoantibodies target adrenal cortex (21-hydroxylase antibodies) and thyroid (anti-TPO). HLA-DR3 and DR4 are strong genetic risk factors for autoimmune endocrine disease.

Addison features: ↓cortisol → ↑ACTH (which stimulates MSH → hyperpigmentation) + ↓aldosterone → hypotension + salt-craving.

APS-2 can also include type 1 diabetes. All three = full APS-2 triad.
Integrates L2 (adrenal zones, cortisol/ACTH/aldosterone) with L5 (Hashimoto mechanism). APS-2 / Schmidt syndrome = Step 1 favorite autoimmune endocrine combination.
Vignette 7Lectures 3 + 5 — Sex Steroids + Thyroid Binding
A woman starts oral estrogen therapy. Her total T4 rises significantly, but her free T4 and TSH remain normal. She feels completely euthyroid.
→ Explain why total T4 rose without any change in thyroid function. What protein is involved?
✅ Answer
Thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG) increase from estrogen. Estrogen → ↑hepatic TBG synthesis → more TBG in blood → binds more T4 → ↑total T4. But the increase in TBG "mops up" free T4 transiently → slight ↑TSH → thyroid makes slightly more T4 → new equilibrium where total T4 is higher but free T4 is normal.

Analogy: SHBG (from L3) binds sex steroids; TBG binds thyroid hormones. Both are carrier proteins — what matters is the FREE fraction.

Clinically important: pregnancy (high estrogen) causes same TBG rise → hypothyroid patients need 25-30% more levothyroxine. Do not misinterpret elevated total T4 as hyperthyroidism in pregnant or estrogen-taking patients.
Integrates L3 (carrier proteins, SHBG concept) with L5 (T4 binding, TBG, free vs total hormone). Tests whether you know the carrier protein principle applies to thyroid hormones too.
Vignette 8Lectures 1 + 3 + 4 — GPCR + Sex Steroids + ADH
Oxytocin and ADH are both synthesized in the hypothalamus and stored in the posterior pituitary. A drug selectively activates only Gq-coupled receptors. Predict: (1) which ADH receptor would be activated, (2) what would happen to blood pressure, (3) would water reabsorption increase?
→ Explain using receptor-specific G-protein coupling.
✅ Answer
(1) The V1 receptor is Gq-coupled. Selective Gq activation → V1 activated on vascular smooth muscle.

(2) Blood pressure would increase: Gq → PLC → IP₃ → ↑Ca²⁺ → smooth muscle contraction → vasoconstriction → ↑SVR → ↑BP.

(3) Water reabsorption would NOT increase: water reabsorption requires V2 receptor → Gs → cAMP → AQP-2. The drug activates Gq, not Gs, so V2-mediated AQP-2 insertion does not occur. Urine remains dilute.

Oxytocin receptor is also Gq → the drug would also stimulate oxytocin receptor → uterine contraction risk.

This is a classic "can you separate V1 and V2 by G-protein" question.
Pure L1 (Gq vs Gs signaling) + L4 (V1 vs V2 receptor consequences) integration. Tests G-protein pharmacology prediction, not memorization.
Vignette 9Lectures 2 + 3 — 17α-hydroxylase + Sex Development
Twin siblings — one 46,XX and one 46,XY — both present at age 17 with primary amenorrhea/lack of male development, hypertension, and ↑aldosterone. Neither has developed secondary sex characteristics. Both have undetectable androgens and estrogens.
→ What enzyme is deficient? Why do BOTH karyotypes lack sex development? Why is there HTN?
✅ Answer
Pregnenolone → [17α-OH blocked] → Cannot make 17-OH-pregnenolone or 17-OH-progesterone
→ No DHEA, no androstenedione, no testosterone, no estradiol
→ Progesterone/DOC/Corticosterone pathway still open → ↑Aldosterone → HTN
17α-hydroxylase deficiency.

XY (46,XY): No testosterone → no masculinization of external genitalia or brain → female phenotype. Testes present but non-functional. No uterus (MIF still secreted by Sertoli cells). No pubic hair (androgens absent).

XX (46,XX): No estrogen → no feminization → no breast development, no menstrual cycle. Ovaries present but non-functional. Primary amenorrhea.

Both: ↑aldosterone (mineralocorticoid pathway is intact and upregulated) → HTN + hypokalemia. ↓Cortisol → ↑ACTH.
The classic "both sexes affected equally" CAH. Compare: 21-OH affects XX more visibly (virilization); 17α-OH affects both equally (no sex development). This distinction is HY Step 1.
Vignette 10Lectures 4 + 5 + 1 — Full Integration Boss Vignette
A 62-year-old woman with small cell lung cancer (SCLC) develops confusion, hyponatremia (Na⁺ 118), and seizures. She also has new-onset hypothyroidism (↑TSH, ↓free T4) and her cortisol is low with high ACTH. She is not on any medications. Urine sodium is 45 (elevated), urine osmolality 620.
→ (1) What is causing the hyponatremia? (2) How does SCLC cause this? (3) What do the cortisol/ACTH findings suggest as a separate diagnosis? (4) Sequence your treatment plan and explain why order matters.
✅ Boss-Level Answer
(1) SIADH — low serum Na/Osm + high urine Na/Osm = inappropriate ADH secretion causing free water retention → dilutional hyponatremia.

(2) SCLC mechanism: Small cell lung cancer cells are neuroendocrine and can ectopically secrete ADH (vasopressin) — this is non-osmotic, non-physiologic ADH → V2 → Gs → cAMP → AQP-2 → water retention regardless of serum osmolality. SCLC is the classic paraneoplastic SIADH.

(3) Cortisol low + ACTH high = primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison disease) — possibly from adrenal metastasis (SCLC commonly metastasizes to adrenal glands) or autoimmune. Alternatively, ACTH can also be ectopically produced by SCLC (Cushing syndrome) but here ACTH is high without cortisol response, suggesting primary adrenal failure.

(4) Treatment order (critical):
Step 1: Hydrocortisone first — before treating hyponatremia. Adrenal insufficiency independently causes hyponatremia (↓aldosterone → Na loss; ↓cortisol → ↑ADH). If you rapidly correct Na without addressing the adrenal insufficiency, you risk osmotic demyelination syndrome.
Step 2: Thyroid hormone (levothyroxine) — after steroids are on board (steroids prevent adrenal crisis precipitated by T4 replacement).
Step 3: Fluid restriction ± hypertonic saline for SIADH — raise Na slowly (max 8-10 mEq/L per 24h).
Step 4: Address underlying SCLC.
This integrates L1 (receptor-signaling), L4 (ADH/SIADH, V2-Gs-cAMP), L5 (hypothyroidism/TBG), and L2 (cortisol/ACTH feedback, adrenal insufficiency). This is a Step 1 Boss-level question requiring you to manage multiple simultaneous endocrine crises in sequence.
