BRS Physiology Ch. 7
Q1
A 28-year-old woman presents with secondary amenorrhea, galactorrhea, and headaches. Serum prolactin is 280 ng/mL (normal <25). MRI shows a 12 mm pituitary adenoma. What is the most appropriate initial treatment?
A) Transsphenoidal surgery
B) Radiation therapy
C) Cabergoline (dopamine agonist)
D) Bromocriptine only
E) Octreotide
EXPLANATION
Prolactinoma (macroadenoma) — first-line is dopamine agonist therapy. Cabergoline is preferred over bromocriptine (longer half-life, twice weekly dosing, better tolerated, more effective at tumor shrinkage). Even macroadenomas with mass effect respond well to medical therapy. Surgery is reserved for medical failure, intolerance, or patient preference. Dopamine agonists restore dopamine → ↓prolactin → restore menses and normalize galactorrhea.
TEACHING POINT
Prolactinoma: dopamine agonist FIRST (not surgery). Cabergoline preferred. Even macroadenomas respond to medical therapy.
Q2
Which hypothalamic nucleus is the primary source of dopamine that inhibits prolactin secretion via the tuberoinfundibular dopamine pathway?
A) Paraventricular nucleus
B) Supraoptic nucleus
C) Arcuate nucleus
D) Ventromedial nucleus
E) Lateral hypothalamic area
EXPLANATION
The arcuate nucleus (also called the infundibular nucleus) is the major source of tuberoinfundibular dopamine neurons that project to the median eminence → portal system → anterior pituitary → inhibit lactotrophs. Dopamine = PIF (prolactin-inhibiting factor). Loss of this pathway (stalk compression, antipsychotics) → hyperprolactinemia.
TEACHING POINT
Arcuate nucleus = source of TIDA (tuberoinfundibular dopamine) → primary prolactin inhibitor. Antipsychotics block D2 → remove inhibition → ↑prolactin.
Q3
A patient being treated with risperidone (antipsychotic) develops galactorrhea and menstrual irregularities. What is the mechanism?
A) Direct stimulation of lactotrophs
B) Blockade of dopamine D2 receptors on lactotrophs → removes tonic inhibition → hyperprolactinemia
C) Stimulation of TRH release
D) Direct suppression of estrogen synthesis
E) Stimulation of oxytocin release
EXPLANATION
Antipsychotics (D2 receptor antagonists) block dopamine (PIF) action on lactotrophs → remove tonic inhibition → hyperprolactinemia → galactorrhea, amenorrhea, infertility. This is drug-induced hyperprolactinemia. Treatment: switch to prolactin-sparing antipsychotic (quetiapine, clozapine, aripiprazole) rather than adding cabergoline (which may worsen psychosis).
TEACHING POINT
Drug-induced hyperprolactinemia: D2 antagonists (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, domperidone). Management: switch to prolactin-sparing agent, not cabergoline.
Q4
The mid-cycle LH surge is triggered by rising estrogen during the late follicular phase. This is an example of which type of feedback?
A) Negative long-loop feedback
B) Negative short-loop feedback
C) Positive long-loop feedback
D) Ultrashort feedback
E) No feedback — this is an independent neuroendocrine event
EXPLANATION
Mid-cycle: estrogen rises from growing follicle → when estrogen reaches threshold (>200 pg/mL for ~48 hours) → switches to POSITIVE feedback → stimulates GnRH and acts directly on gonadotrophs → massive LH surge → ovulation. This positive feedback is unique — most other steroid hormone effects on the HPx axis are negative. After ovulation, progesterone + estrogen restore negative feedback.
TEACHING POINT
Positive feedback exception: sustained high estrogen → LH surge. This only occurs mid-cycle. All other estrogen effects on HPG axis are negative feedback.
Q5
TRH (thyrotropin-releasing hormone) stimulates which TWO anterior pituitary hormones?
A) TSH and GH
B) TSH and ACTH
C) TSH and prolactin
D) Prolactin and LH
E) TSH and FSH
EXPLANATION
TRH → thyrotrophs (TSH) AND lactotrophs (prolactin). This dual stimulation explains why primary hypothyroidism can cause hyperprolactinemia and galactorrhea — elevated TRH (from low T4 feedback) → ↑TSH AND ↑prolactin → galactorrhea + amenorrhea. Treatment: levothyroxine (corrects TSH and prolactin) — NOT cabergoline. This is a classic board exam trick.
TEACHING POINT
TRH stimulates TSH + prolactin. Hypothyroidism → ↑TRH → ↑prolactin → galactorrhea. Treat with levothyroxine, not cabergoline/dopamine agonist.
Q6
GnRH must be secreted in a pulsatile fashion to maintain LH and FSH secretion. What happens when GnRH is administered continuously (non-pulsatile)?
A) Increased LH and FSH secretion
B) Downregulation of GnRH receptors → decreased LH and FSH
C) Selective increase in FSH only
D) Increased testosterone and estrogen
E) No change in LH or FSH
EXPLANATION
Continuous (non-pulsatile) GnRH → downregulation of GnRH receptors on gonadotrophs → reduced LH and FSH → medical castration. This is the principle behind GnRH agonists (leuprolide, goserelin): initial flare (first 2 weeks) → then receptor downregulation → ↓LH/FSH/sex steroids. Used for: prostate cancer, endometriosis, precocious puberty, uterine fibroids.
TEACHING POINT
Pulsatile GnRH = stimulates gonadotrophs. Continuous GnRH = downregulates receptors = medical castration. GnRH agonist paradox: stimulate first, then suppress.
Q7
ACTH is derived from a larger precursor molecule, POMC. Which OTHER peptides are co-secreted from corticotrophs along with ACTH?
A) TSH and LH
B) β-endorphin and MSH (melanocyte-stimulating hormone)
C) Prolactin and GH
D) Oxytocin and vasopressin
E) Somatostatin and CRH
EXPLANATION
POMC (pro-opiomelanocortin) is cleaved by prohormone convertases → ACTH, β-LPH (→β-endorphin), and γ-MSH. In Addison's disease or Nelson's syndrome: excess ACTH and MSH → hyperpigmentation (MSH activates MC1R on melanocytes). β-endorphin = endogenous opioid. This cleavage varies by tissue — pituitary vs intermediate lobe vs hypothalamus.
TEACHING POINT
POMC → ACTH + MSH + β-endorphin. High ACTH states (Addison, Nelson) also have high MSH → hyperpigmentation. This explains skin darkening in primary adrenal insufficiency.
Q8
A couple presents for infertility evaluation. The female partner has normal FSH and LH but her doctor suspects GnRH pulsatility is disturbed. What condition would explain this?
A) Hyperthyroidism
B) Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (FHA) from chronic stress/low body weight
C) Pituitary adenoma
D) Polycystic ovary syndrome
E) Turner syndrome
EXPLANATION
FHA: chronic stress, low body weight (BMI <18), excessive exercise → ↑CRH → disrupts GnRH pulse generator → ↓LH pulse frequency and amplitude → anovulation. LH and FSH may be low-normal (not absent — the GnRH generator is partially functional). Treatment: address underlying cause (restore weight, reduce stress). Pulsatile GnRH can restore fertility.
TEACHING POINT
FHA: stress + energy deficit → ↑CRH → ↓GnRH pulsatility → ↓LH/FSH. Distinguished from pituitary failure by normal GnRH stimulation test response.
Q9
Which anterior pituitary cell type is most abundant and produces the largest pituitary tumor type?
A) Lactotrophs
B) Thyrotrophs
C) Somatotrophs
D) Corticotrophs
E) Gonadotrophs
EXPLANATION
Somatotrophs are the most abundant anterior pituitary cell type (~50% of cells). They produce GH-secreting adenomas (acromegaly) which are the second most common functioning pituitary adenoma (after prolactinomas). Nonfunctional adenomas (gonadotroph origin) are the most common overall. Lactotrophs (prolactinomas) are the most common functioning adenoma.
TEACHING POINT
Most abundant anterior pituitary cells: somatotrophs. Most common functioning adenoma: prolactinoma. Most common pituitary adenoma overall: non-functioning (null cell/gonadotroph).
Q10
A female patient undergoes bilateral oophorectomy. What happens to serum LH and FSH levels within weeks?
A) Both decrease due to stress
B) LH increases, FSH remains normal
C) Both increase markedly
D) LH decreases, FSH increases
E) Both remain unchanged
EXPLANATION
Oophorectomy removes estrogen and progesterone → loss of negative feedback on hypothalamus and pituitary → ↑GnRH → ↑LH and ↑FSH (FSH rises more due to loss of inhibin B from granulosa cells). This is the basis of menopause physiology — as ovarian reserve declines, FSH rises first (inhibin B falls before estrogen), then LH rises as estrogen also falls.
TEACHING POINT
Remove ovaries → remove negative feedback → LH ↑ + FSH ↑. FSH rises first in menopause (loss of inhibin). Useful mnemonic: castration → freed pituitary → both gonadotropins surge.
Q1
A 30-year-old woman has elevated serum prolactin. To determine whether this represents a prolactinoma or stalk compression, which of the following prolactin values most strongly suggests a prolactinoma (rather than stalk compression)?
A) Prolactin 35 ng/mL
B) Prolactin 80 ng/mL
C) Prolactin 220 ng/mL (with 15 mm adenoma)
D) Prolactin 400 ng/mL (with 25 mm macroadenoma)
E) Prolactin 50 ng/mL
EXPLANATION
The magnitude of prolactin elevation helps distinguish prolactinoma from stalk effect. Stalk compression (any cause — non-functioning adenoma, craniopharyngioma) typically elevates prolactin to 25-150 ng/mL due to loss of dopamine. True prolactinomas typically: microadenoma →100-500 ng/mL; macroadenoma → >500 ng/mL. A prolactin of 400 ng/mL with a large macroadenoma strongly suggests true prolactin secretion. Key: if prolactin is only mildly elevated with a large tumor, consider stalk compression.
TEACHING POINT
Prolactin >200-500 ng/mL with macroadenoma = true prolactinoma. Modest elevation (25-150 ng/mL) with any mass = likely stalk effect.
Q2
Leuprolide acetate is prescribed for endometriosis. How does its mechanism of action lead to a reduction in estrogen levels?
A) Blocks estrogen receptors in the uterus
B) Inhibits ovarian steroidogenesis directly
C) Continuous GnRH receptor stimulation → downregulation → ↓LH/FSH → ↓estrogen
D) Inhibits FSH at the gonadotroph level
E) Stimulates somatostatin to suppress FSH
EXPLANATION
Leuprolide is a GnRH agonist with prolonged half-life. Given continuously → initially stimulates GnRH receptors (flare) → chronic receptor overstimulation → receptor downregulation/desensitization → ↓LH and FSH → ↓ovarian estrogen and progesterone. Creates a "medical oophorectomy." Side effects: menopausal symptoms (hot flashes, bone loss). Combined with add-back therapy or bisphosphonates for long-term use.
TEACHING POINT
Leuprolide paradox: GnRH agonist → suppresses sex steroids after initial flare. Use for: endometriosis, fibroids, precocious puberty, prostate cancer.
Q3
Which of the following best describes the regulation of FSH vs LH secretion from gonadotrophs?
A) Both are regulated identically by GnRH alone
B) FSH is primarily regulated by inhibin B (suppresses FSH selectively) and activin (stimulates FSH); LH is primarily regulated by GnRH pulse frequency
C) LH is regulated by inhibin; FSH by GnRH
D) FSH and LH are regulated by separate releasing hormones
E) LH is primarily regulated by follistatin
EXPLANATION
FSH and LH diverge in regulation: GnRH regulates both, but FSH is uniquely regulated by: Inhibin B (from granulosa/Sertoli cells) → selectively suppresses FSH without affecting LH. Activin → stimulates FSH. Follistatin → suppresses FSH by binding activin. LH is primarily regulated by GnRH pulse frequency (fast pulses → LH; slow pulses → FSH). This differential regulation allows selective FSH rise in early menopause before LH rises.
TEACHING POINT
Inhibin B → suppresses FSH ONLY. This explains why FSH rises before LH in menopause (inhibin B falls with declining follicle number).
Q4
A patient with a non-functioning pituitary macroadenoma (no hormone secretion) undergoes surgical resection but has incomplete removal. Post-operatively, which hormonal deficiency is most likely to occur last (i.e., most resistant to damage)?
A) ACTH
B) TSH
C) GH
D) LH/FSH
E) Prolactin
EXPLANATION
Order of hormone loss with pituitary damage/compression (most sensitive → most resistant): GH (first, most sensitive) → FSH/LH → TSH → ACTH → Prolactin (LAST). Prolactin is LAST because it is tonically inhibited — damage that removes dopamine inhibition actually INCREASES prolactin. GH is first because somatotrophs are most numerous and most sensitive to compression/ischemia.
TEACHING POINT
Pituitary hormone loss order (most to least sensitive): GH → FSH/LH → TSH → ACTH → Prolactin last (because losing dopamine inhibition = prolactin rises). Screen GH first in suspected hypopituitarism.
Q5
ACTH has a circadian rhythm with peak levels occurring at which time?
A) Midnight
B) Early morning (6-8 AM)
C) Noon
D) Late afternoon
E) ACTH has no circadian rhythm
EXPLANATION
ACTH (and cortisol) follow a circadian rhythm: PEAK in early morning (6-8 AM) → nadir at midnight. This is driven by suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) → CRH → ACTH → cortisol. Clinical implications: (1) Morning cortisol is the reference standard. (2) Late-night salivary cortisol should be low — if elevated, suggests Cushing syndrome. (3) Steroid replacement should mimic this rhythm (higher dose in AM).
TEACHING POINT
Cortisol/ACTH peak: early morning. Nadir: midnight. Late-night salivary cortisol elevation = Cushing screening test. Give steroid replacement in AM to mimic physiology.
