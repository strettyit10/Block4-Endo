   What two tests may be used to differentiate between Cushing disease and ectopic (paraneoplastic) ACTH secretion?

- High-dose dexamethasone suppression test
- CRH stimulation test
- High-dose dexamethasone test inhibits ACTH-secreting pituitary adenomas, but NOT ectopic ACTH-secreting tumors

- CRH stimulation test increases ACTH production in pituitary adenomas, but fails to increase ACTH production with ectopic tumors

30.1 [...] syndrome is an enlargement of existing ACTH-secreting pituitary adenoma after bilateral adrenalectomy for refractory Cushing syndrome
   Nelson syndrome is an enlargement of existing ACTH-secreting pituitary adenoma after bilateral adrenalectomy for refractory Cushing syndrome
- AKA postadrenalectomy syndrome

- Sudden decrease in cortisol increases ACTH secretion via loss of negative feedback; leads to enlargement of preexisting but undetected pituitary adenoma

- Patient must take glucocorticoid & mineralocorticoids for the rest of their life


1) Indications include primary Cushing syndrome due to bilateral adrenal disease or severe secondary Cushing syndrome refractory to treatment
2) Bilateral adrenalectomy causes sudden drop in cortisol
3) Loss of negative feedback from cortisol
4) Increased corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) production
5) Enlargement of preexisting but undetected pituitary adenoma
6) Symptoms manifest (e.g., hyperpigmentation, headaches, & bitemporal hemianopsia)
