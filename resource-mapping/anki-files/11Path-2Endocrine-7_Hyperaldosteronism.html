   Secondary hyperaldosteronism is seen in patients with renovascular hypertension due to independent activation of the RAAS

3.1

- [...]tension
- [...]natremia
- [...]kalemia
- [Acid-base disturbance]

  
Hyperaldosteronism presents with the following findings:

- Hypertension
- Hypernatremia
- Hypokalemia
- Metabolic alkalosis

- Due to aldosterone escape mechanism, there is usually no clinically significant edema or hypernatremia in primary hyperaldosteronism (more common in secondary hyperaldosteronism)

- Goljan: May present with hypocalcemia due to alkalosis (albumin becomes more negative and binds Ca2+) which leads to tetany

What is the state of the adrenal glands in Cushing syndrome due to primary adrenal adenoma, hyperplasia, or carcinoma?

Atrophy of the uninvolved gland; hyperplasia of the involved gland

Thus can also present with pseudohyperaldosteronism
5.1
[...] hyperaldosteronism is characterized by high aldosterone and low renin

  
Primary hyperaldosteronism is characterized by high aldosterone and low renin

- This results in resistant hypertension; renin is downregulated via high blood pressure

- NO direct edema due to aldosterone escape mechanism (aldosterone upregulates ENaC, increased BV causes atria to release ANP to downregulate ENaC)
6.1 [...] hyperaldosteronism is seen in patients with juxtaglomerular cell tumor due to independent activation of the RAAS
   Secondary hyperaldosteronism is seen in patients with juxtaglomerular cell tumor due to independent activation of the RAAS
- Results in severe hypertension that is difficult to control

- These secrete renin (hence AKA reninoma), thus you also have ATII upregulation as well as aldosterone (failure of aldosterone escape)
7.1 Secondary hyperaldosteronism is due to activation of [...] system
   Secondary hyperaldosteronism is due to activation of renin-angiotensin system
- Seen in patients with renovascular hypertension (renal artery stenosis), juxtaglomerular cell tumors, and edema (cirrhosis, heart failure, nephrotic syndrome)

- EDEMA present due to NO aldosterone escape mechanism (even if ANP rises, ATII is still present increasing Na+ reabsorption through Na+ / H+ exchanger)
8.1
[...] hyperaldosteronism is characterized by high aldosterone and high renin

  
Secondary hyperaldosteronism is characterized by high aldosterone and high renin

- Seen in patients with renovascular hypertension and juxtaglomerular cell tumor (due to independent activation of renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), as well as causes of edema (cirrhosis, heart failure, nephrotic syndrome)

- EDEMA present due to NO aldosterone escape mechanism (even if ANP is elevated, ATII is still increasing Na+ reabsorption via Na+ / H+ exchanger)

- vs. primary hyperaldosteronism, which would be characterized by high aldosterone and low renin

No

10.1 Primary hyperaldosteronism activates "aldosterone escape", which leads to a steady state of [sodium levels], [potassium levels] and [volume levels]
   Primary hyperaldosteronism activates "aldosterone escape", which leads to a steady state of normonatremia, hypokalemia and hypervolemia
- Excess aldosterone → increased Na+ reabsorption → negative luminal charge, which pulls K+ and H+ into urine → hypokalemia + metabolic alkalosis

- Despite the increase in Na+ reabsorption, hypernatremia and pedal edema are rarely observed due to increased intravascular volume, which increases renal blood flow (resulting in pressure natriuresis and normal sodium levels)
11.1 The diagnosis of primary hyperaldosteronism may be confirmed by a(n) oral sodium loading test showing elevated [...]
   The diagnosis of primary hyperaldosteronism may be confirmed by a(n) oral sodium loading test showing elevated urine aldosterone
- Patients adhere to a high-sodium diet for 3 days. 24-hour urine measurements of aldosterone are elevated (> 12 mcg/day) in patients with primary hyperaldosteronism because of a failure to suppress aldosterone secretion

- A positive test requires further adrenal imaging

- This card was recently updated to reflect more accurate terminology and test findings (July 2025)
12.1 Primary hyperaldosteronism may present with [...] renal blood flow and [...] glomerular filtration rate
  Primary hyperaldosteronism may present with increased renal blood flow and increased glomerular filtration rate
Due to arterial hypertension and hypersecretion of aldosterone
13.1 Primary hyperaldosteronism initially causes severe volume [...] and [...]tension
   Primary hyperaldosteronism initially causes severe volume overload and hypertension
- Due to sodium and water retention

- Primary hyperaldosteronism eventually activates aldosterone escape via pressure natriuresis and increased ANP, leading to a steady state of normonatremia, hypokalemia and hypervolemia
14.1 Congestive heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, and excessive peripheral edema may cause [...] hyperaldosteronism
   Congestive heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, and excessive peripheral edema may cause secondary hyperaldosteronism

Primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome) is most commonly caused by bilateral adrenal hyperplasia (60%) or aldosteronoma (30%)

Note: some sources define Conn syndrome as primary hyperaldosteronism regardless of cause while others specify this denotes an aldosteronoma

   Treatment of Conn syndrome consists of administration of a(n) aldosterone antagonist, such as spironolactone, followed by removal of the aldosterone-secreting tumor
18.1
Conn syndrome presents with hypertension, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis due to [...] aldosterone levels

  
Conn syndrome presents with hypertension, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis due to increased aldosterone levels

- Also have decreased renin levels; increased ECF volume causes increased renal perfusion pressure, which inhibits renin secretion

- These electrolyte imbalances may cause paresthesias and muscle weakness in some patients




19.1
Nephrotic syndrome results in [lab finding], which manifests as pitting edema on physical exam and secondary hyperaldosteronism

  
Nephrotic syndrome results in hypoalbuminemia, which manifests as pitting edema on physical exam and secondary hyperaldosteronism

- Edema leads to a decreased effective circulating volume, causing a secondary hyperaldosteronism which leads to salt retention and exacerbating the edema (generated by decreased oncotic pressure)

- Also manifests as periorbital edema (thus can present clinically as a patient who thinks they are having an allergic reaction)

22.1 Primary hyperaldosteronism does not directly cause edema or hypernatremia due to [...], which occurs through pressure natriuresis and increased ANP secretion
   Primary hyperaldosteronism does not directly cause edema or hypernatremia due to aldosterone escape, which occurs through pressure natriuresis and increased ANP secretion
- Aldosterone escape mechanism/no edema and ↔ Na+: aldosterone causes retention of Na+/water, which induces ANP secretion, causing increased excretion of Na+/water

- Pressure natriuresis: ↑ volume = ↑ RBF = ↑ Na+/H2O secretion
