What is the treatment for diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state (HHS)?

- IV fluids (restore blood volume)
- IV insulin (restore glucose and stop ketogenesis)
- K+ (to replete intracellular stores)

- Fluids: peed out fluid with glucose
- Potassium: Replete if levels ≤ 5.2 mEq/L
- Despite extracellular hyperkalemia (due to low insulin), must add [K] because total body potassium is low; we peed out the [K], so re-adding insulin can shift [K] back into the cells and cause hypokalemia

- When treating dehydrated, hyperglycemic patients with insulin and intravenous fluids, be cautious as these interventions can cause a sudden drop in serum potassium levels.    How do total body K+ levels typically change in patients with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS)?

Decreased

NOTE: Despite elevated serum potassium, total body potassium may be low

- When treated with insulin, K+ rapidly shifts from the extracellular space back into cells potentially resulting in a dangerously low serum K+ (hypokalemia)

- Insulin shifts K+ into the cells. Low insulin (T1DM) or insulin resistance (T2DM) may lead to to high serum (extracellular) K+ and low intracellular/total body K+

- This may be further exacerbated by the K+-H+ exchanger as it tries to compensate in an acidotic state (e.g., DKA)
