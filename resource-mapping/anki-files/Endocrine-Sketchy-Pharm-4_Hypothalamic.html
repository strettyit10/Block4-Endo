   What are the pharmacological treatment options for nocturnal enuresis?

First-line: desmopressin
Second-line: imipramine
- Pharmacotherapy is typically reserved for children ≥ 7 y.o. if nonpharmacological options are unsuccessful or if more rapid improvement is desired

- Desmopressin decreases urine production during sleep (ADH analog)

- Desmopressin preferred due to side effects of imipramine

- ≥ 5 y.o. → rule out organic causes → lifestyle modification and behavioral training → enuresis alarm ± pharmacotherapy (desmopressin > imipramine)

   What is the next step in treatment for primary nocturnal enuresis (bed wetting) after unsuccessful lifestyle modification and behavioral training?

Enuresis alarm ± oral desmopressin
*Pharmacotherapy is typically reserved for children ≥ 7 yo if nonpharmacological options are unsuccessful or if more rapid improvement is desired

   What is the initial treatment for a macroprolactinoma (> 10 mm) or symptomatic microprolactinoma?

Dopamine agonist (e.g., cabergoline, bromocriptine)

- Cabergoline is generally preferred over bromocriptine due to its superior efficacy and side-effect profile

- > 90% of patients respond to medical therapy (reduction in prolactin levels and reduced tumor size)

- Consider transsphenoidal resection for patients who fail to respond to dopamine agonist treatment or who have very large tumors (> 3 cm)
