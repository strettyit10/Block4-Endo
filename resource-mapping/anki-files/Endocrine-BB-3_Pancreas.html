How does serum K+ change in a patient with diabetic ketoacidosis?

Normal / increased

NOTE: Despite elevated serum potassium, total body potassium may be low

- When treated with insulin, K+ rapidly shifts from the extracellular space back into cells potentially resulting in a dangerously low serum K+ (hypokalemia)

- Insulin shifts K+ into the cells. Low or absent insulin in T1DM may lead to high serum (extracellular) K+ and low intracellular/total body K+. This may be further exacerbated by the K+-H+ exchanger as it tries to compensate in an acidotic state (e.g., DKA)

   Hypoglycemia can be precipitated by exercise & alcohol in patients with insulin-treated diabetes -- why?

Exogenous insulin continues to suck glucose into cells on top of exercising skeletal muscles

Metabolization of alcohol in the liver suppresses gluconeogenesis

Exercise: normally the body ↓ secretion of insulin during exercise to maintain blood glucose, but this mechanism is ineffective when exogenous insulin is present

Alcohol: increased NADH/NAD+ ratio causes increased conversion of oxaloacetate → malate, thus preventing gluconeogenesis
