Subacute granulomatous thyroiditis (de Quervain) may initially present with hyperthyroidism, which typically is followed by a brief hypothyroid state

- Thyroid symptoms usually mild; this is permanent in ~15% of cases

- During the transient hyperthyroid phase, radioiodine uptake is decreased due to low TSH levels

- Typical pattern of thyroiditis (initially hyper spilling
out T3/T4, then transitioning to burnout hypo)

Hashimoto thyroiditis

   What is the likely outcome in most cases of subacute thyroiditis?

Complete recovery (return to euthyroid state)

Subacute thyroiditis consist of two main subtypes:
1) Subacute granulomatous thyroiditis AKA de Quervain thyroiditis
2) Subacute lymphocytic thyroiditis AKA silent or painless thyroiditis

- Most cases self-resolve within 1 year; permanent hypothyroidism occurs in ∼15% of cases
