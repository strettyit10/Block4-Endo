Nuclear Receptors (Hydrophobic Hormones)

  • Nuclear receptor superfamily: steroid/thyroid/retinoid receptors — all are TRANSCRIPTION FACTORS
  • Structure: ligand-binding domain (~27kD) + DNA-binding domain (zinc fingers, ~10kD)
  • Mechanism: hormone diffuses → binds receptor → receptor dimerizes → nucleus → binds HRE → activates or represses transcription
  • RXR dimerizes with: TR (thyroid), RAR (retinoic acid), VDR (vitamin D), PPAR
  • Steroid receptors (GR, MR, AR, PR, ER): cytoplasmic without hormone, homodimerize
  • TR unique: bound to DNA even WITHOUT hormone → REPRESSING transcription until T3 arrives
🎯 HOW THIS IS TESTED — Unit 1
  • Hydrophilic = surface receptor; Hydrophobic = nuclear receptor — tested frequently
  • Glycoprotein hormones (TSH/FSH/LH/hCG): SAME α-subunit, different β-subunit = specificity
  • Scatchard plot: straight negative slope = single receptor class. X-intercept = Bmax, slope = −1/Kd
  • TR/RAR/VDR: bound to DNA without hormone, REPRESSING transcription — hormone de-represses + activates
  • Pregnancy ↑ TBG → ↑ total T4 but FREE T4 normal → euthyroid (tested as "normal thyroid in pregnancy")
⚠️ TEST TRAPS — Unit 1
  • Epinephrine = AA derivative but acts like a PEPTIDE (surface receptor), NOT like thyroid hormone
  • Thyroid hormones = AA derivatives but HYDROPHOBIC → nuclear receptor (exception to "AA derivatives = surface")
  • Nuclear receptors for steroids are ACTIVATED by hormone. TR is REPRESSED without hormone (activated by T3)
  • Dopamine = neurotransmitter AND endocrine hormone (prolactin inhibiting factor)
📝 MINI Q 1
A hormone circulates at very low concentrations bound to a carrier protein. Its receptor is found in the nucleus and represses transcription in the absence of ligand. Which hormone is most likely described?
A) Insulin
B) Cortisol
C) Triiodothyronine (T3) ✓
D) Epinephrine
E) Aldosterone
✅ C — T3
T3 is an AA derivative that is hydrophobic, travels on TBG, and its receptor (TR) is bound to TREs even without hormone — repressing transcription until T3 binds.
⚠️ Trap: Cortisol also uses a nuclear receptor, but its receptor is cytoplasmic without hormone and does NOT repress DNA in the hormone-absent state. Only TR/RAR/VDR do that.
📝 MINI Q 2
A patient's serum total T4 is elevated during her second trimester of pregnancy. Her free T4 and TSH are both normal. What is the most likely explanation?
A) Graves' disease with normal TSH feedback
B) Thyroid adenoma
C) Estrogen-induced increase in TBG levels ✓
D) Placental hCG cross-reacting with TSH receptor
E) Hashimoto's thyroiditis hyperthyroid phase
✅ C — Estrogen ↑ TBG
Pregnancy → estrogen → ↑TBG → more bound T4 → ↑total T4. But free T4 is unchanged, TSH normal = euthyroid. Normal pregnancy finding.
⚠️ Trap: Graves' would suppress TSH. hCG cross-reactivity would also suppress TSH. Normal TSH + normal free T4 = normal thyroid function.
⚡ HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY — Unit 1
  • 3 classes: AA derivatives (hydrophilic=catecholamines, hydrophobic=TH), Peptides (hydrophilic), Steroids (hydrophobic)
  • Hydrophilic → surface receptor (GPCR or RTK) → 2nd messengers → fast effects
  • Hydrophobic → nuclear receptor → alters transcription → slow, sustained effects
  • RXR dimerizes with: TR, RAR, VDR, PPAR
  • Glycoprotein hormones: SAME α-subunit, DIFFERENT β-subunit = specificity
  • Only FREE hormone is active; carrier proteins extend half-life
  • TR: represses DNA without hormone; T3 binding = de-repression + activation

🎯 HOW THIS IS TESTED — Unit 2
  • 21β-Hydroxylase deficiency is THE most tested enzyme defect. Buzzwords: virilized female newborn, ↑17-OH-progesterone, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypotension, ↑ACTH
  • 11β-Hydroxylase: same virilization BUT with HYPERTENSION (11-DOC) — the distinguishing feature
  • 17α-Hydroxylase: NO sex steroids → sexual infantilism + hypertension + hypokalemia (opposite of 21β)
  • 11β-HSD2 deficiency or licorice inhibition: syndrome like hyperaldosteronism but LOW aldosterone (cortisol acting on MR)
  • Lab pattern: ↑ACTH + ↑17-OH-progesterone → 21β-hydroxylase deficiency
⚠️ TEST TRAPS — Unit 2
  • Glomerulosa: NO 17α-hydroxylase → cannot make cortisol or androgens
  • Fasciculata: NO aldosterone synthase → cannot make aldosterone
  • 17α-hydroxylase and 17,20-lyase are the SAME enzyme (CYP17A1) — cytochrome B5 determines lyase activity
  • CYP11B1 vs CYP11B2: B2 (glomerulosa only) also has 18-hydroxylase/oxidase → makes aldosterone
  • Licorice (glycyrrhizinic acid) inhibits 11β-HSD2 → apparent mineralocorticoid excess: HTN + hypokalemia + LOW aldosterone
📝 MINI Q 3
A newborn female presents with ambiguous genitalia. Labs show hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypotension, markedly elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone, and elevated ACTH. Which enzyme is most likely deficient?
A) 11β-Hydroxylase
B) 17α-Hydroxylase
C) Aldosterone synthase
D) 21β-Hydroxylase ✓
E) Cholesterol desmolase
✅ D — 21β-Hydroxylase
Classic: 17-OH-progesterone cannot proceed → accumulates + shunts to androgens (virilization). No cortisol → ↑ACTH. No aldosterone → salt wasting (hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypotension).
⚠️ Trap: 11β-Hydroxylase also causes virilization but causes HYPERTENSION due to 11-deoxycorticosterone buildup — the key distinguishing feature.
📝 MINI Q 4
A patient has hypertension, hypokalemia, low plasma renin, and LOW aldosterone. She eats large amounts of licorice. The most likely mechanism involves inhibition of which enzyme?
A) 17α-Hydroxylase
B) Aldosterone synthase
C) 11β-Hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (11β-HSD2) ✓
D) Cholesterol desmolase
E) 21β-Hydroxylase
✅ C — 11β-HSD2
Glycyrrhizinic acid in licorice inhibits 11β-HSD2 → cortisol not inactivated in kidney → cortisol acts on MR → Na+ retention, K+ loss, HTN — despite LOW aldosterone.
⚠️ Trap: Low aldosterone with mineralocorticoid excess symptoms = apparent mineralocorticoid excess (AME). Primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn) would show HIGH aldosterone.
📝 MINI Q 5
A 16-year-old with 46,XY karyotype presents with primary amenorrhea, absent secondary sexual development, hypertension, and hypokalemia. Which enzyme deficiency best explains this?
A) 21β-Hydroxylase
B) 11β-Hydroxylase
C) 17α-Hydroxylase ✓
D) 5α-Reductase
E) Aromatase
✅ C — 17α-Hydroxylase
Blocks cortisol AND sex steroid synthesis. ↑ACTH → pathway shunts to mineralocorticoids (DOC, corticosterone) → HTN + hypokalemia. No sex steroids → sexual infantilism regardless of karyotype.
⚠️ Trap: 46XY with 17α-OH deficiency has female external phenotype (no androgens) but no uterus/tubes (MIF made by testes).
⚡ HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY — Unit 2
  • GFR mnemonic: Glomerulosa=Aldosterone, Fasciculata=Cortisol, Reticularis=androgens
  • All steroids: Cholesterol → Pregnenolone (desmolase, MITOCHONDRIA) → everything else
  • 21β-OH def: ↑17-OH-prog, ↓cortisol, ↓aldosterone, ↑androgens → virilization + salt-wasting + HYPOTENSION
  • 11β-OH def: similar but 11-DOC accumulates → HYPERTENSION (key distinguisher!)
  • 17α-OH def: ↑mineralocorticoids, ↓cortisol, ↓sex steroids → HTN + sexual infantilism
  • 11β-HSD2 protects kidney MR from cortisol. Licorice inhibits it → apparent mineralocorticoid excess

UNIT 3: Sex Steroid Synthesis & Action

Lecturer: Dr. Danielsen | GUSOM Class: Sex Steroid Synthesis and Action

3.1 Androgen Synthesis (Testes)

Testosterone Pathway

  • Cholesterol → Pregnenolone (desmolase)
  • Pregnenolone → DHEA (17α-OH → 17,20-lyase)
  • DHEA → Androstenedione (3β-HSD)
  • Androstenedione → TESTOSTERONE (17β-HSD) ← KEY STEP
  • Made by: LEYDIG CELLS (interstitial cells)
  • Regulated by: LH → cAMP → steroidogenesis
  • Carrier: SHBG ~60%, albumin ~38%, FREE ~2%

Testosterone Derivatives

  • T → DHT (dihydrotestosterone) via 5α-reductase in prostate/genital skin/hair — more potent androgen; required for male external genitalia in utero
  • T → Estradiol via AROMATASE in adipose/brain — how men make estrogens

3.2 Ovarian Steroid Synthesis — Two-Cell Model

THECAL CELLS (LH-dependent)GRANULOSA CELLS (FSH-dependent)
LH → cAMP → androstenedione (primary output). Cannot aromatize. Androgens diffuse into granulosa cells. FSH → ↑aromatase → converts androstenedione → E1 (estrone) and testosterone → E2 (estradiol). Primary estrogen in premenopausal women = 17β-Estradiol (E2)

🎯 HOW THIS IS TESTED — Unit 3
  • MOST TESTED: Ambiguous genitalia — 5α-Reductase def vs AIS: BOTH are 46XY females with testes
  • 5α-Reductase def: virilizes at puberty (testosterone still works). AIS: NEVER virilizes (receptor broken)
  • Postmenopausal estrogen source = adipose aromatase. Obesity → more estrogen → ↑endometrial/breast cancer risk
  • Two-cell model: LH → thecal androgens; FSH → granulosa aromatase. BOTH required for ovarian estrogen synthesis
  • SHBG: obesity ↓SHBG → ↑free testosterone (link to PCOS, type 2 DM)
⚠️ TEST TRAPS — Unit 3
  • Estrogen is NOT female-specific: 80% of male estradiol comes from peripheral aromatization of testosterone
  • Androgens are NOT male-specific: women need androgens for estrogen synthesis (two-cell model)
  • 5α-Reductase def vs AIS: BOTH 46XY with testes and female phenotype. 5α-red can virilize; AIS cannot
  • ABP (seminiferous tubules) = same GENE as SHBG, different glycosylation
  • Estriol = marker of fetal well-being. Low estriol → Down syndrome (triple screen)
📝 MINI Q 6
A 17-year-old presents with primary amenorrhea, normal female external genitalia, no pubic/axillary hair, no uterus on imaging, and a 46,XY karyotype. Serum testosterone is elevated. Most likely diagnosis?
A) 5α-Reductase deficiency
B) Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) ✓
C) Turner syndrome (45X)
D) 17α-Hydroxylase deficiency
E) Müllerian agenesis
✅ B — CAIS
46XY, non-functional AR, testes make testosterone (↑) but it cannot signal. Complete female phenotype. No uterus (MIF made). No pubic/axillary hair (requires androgen action). Primary amenorrhea.
⚠️ Trap: 5α-Reductase def would show virilization at puberty. Turner = 45X. 17α-OH def = LOW testosterone (no sex steroids made).
⚡ HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY — Unit 3
  • Testosterone: Leydig cells; converted to DHT (5α-reductase) or E2 (aromatase)
  • DHT required for male external genitalia in utero AND prostate/beard hair at puberty
  • Two-cell model: Thecal (LH→androgens) + Granulosa (FSH→aromatase→estrogens)
  • Estrogens: E2 (reproductive); E3 (pregnancy, placenta); E1 (postmenopause, fat)
  • 5α-Reductase def: 46XY female at birth → virilizes at puberty. CAIS: 46XY always female, ↑T
  • SHBG ↑ by estrogens/hyperthyroid; ↓ by androgens/obesity → low SHBG = more free T (PCOS)

UNIT 4: Posterior Pituitary Hormones — ADH & Oxytocin

Lecturer: Dr. Sherman | GUSOM Class: Posterior Pituitary Hormones

4.1 Overview

ADH (Vasopressin / AVP)

  • Made in: Supraoptic nuclei of hypothalamus
  • Released from: Posterior pituitary
  • Primary trigger: ↑plasma osmolality (osmoreceptors)
  • Secondary trigger: ↓blood volume (baroreceptors)
  • Osmotic threshold for ADH secretion: ~280 mOsm/kg
  • Osmotic threshold for THIRST: ~290 mOsm/kg (5-10 mOsm HIGHER)

OXYTOCIN

  • Made in: Paraventricular nuclei of hypothalamus
  • Triggers: suckling reflex, uterine distension
  • Acts on: Uterine myometrium + mammary myoepithelial cells
  • Receptor: Gq → IP3/DAG → Ca²⁺ (both uterus and mammary)
  • Receptor ↑ near term → oxytocin augments labor

🎯 HOW THIS IS TESTED — Unit 4
  • DI vignettes: polyuria + polydipsia + dilute urine + hypernatremia → DDAVP test distinguishes central from nephrogenic
  • Central DI causes: head trauma, pituitary surgery, craniopharyngioma, hypoxic brain injury
  • Nephrogenic DI causes: lithium (most common drug), hypercalcemia, hypokalemia, demeclocycline
  • DDAVP: V2-selective, resistant to vasopressinase → treats central AND gestational DI
  • Oxytocin receptor antagonists can't stop premature labor — receptor expression LOW pre-term, HIGH at term
⚠️ TEST TRAPS — Unit 4
  • Osmotic threshold for THIRST is HIGHER than for ADH — body conserves water before making you thirsty
  • Vasopressin = ADH = AVP = arginine vasopressin — all the same molecule
  • Lithium-induced nephrogenic DI: amiloride preferred (blocks Li⁺ entry via ENaC)
  • V1a: vasoconstriction. V2: antidiuresis. V1b: ACTH. Know which does what!
📝 MINI Q 7
3 days post-transsphenoidal pituitary surgery, a patient develops polyuria (5-6 L/day), polydipsia, and serum Na⁺ 152 mEq/L. Urine osmolality 80 mOsm/kg. After intranasal DDAVP, urine osmolality rises to 600 mOsm/kg. Diagnosis?
A) Nephrogenic DI
B) SIADH
C) Central DI ✓
D) Primary polydipsia
E) Gestational DI
✅ C — Central DI
Post-surgical damage to posterior pituitary → ↓ADH production → dilute urine despite hypernatremia. Response to DDAVP (urine concentrates) = CENTRAL DI. Nephrogenic DI would NOT respond to DDAVP.
⚠️ Trap: Primary polydipsia = ↓serum osmolality (drinking too much water). SIADH = concentrated urine + hyponatremia.
📝 MINI Q 8
A woman at 28 weeks gestation presents with polyuria, polydipsia, and Na⁺ 149 mEq/L. Urine osmolality 120 mOsm/kg. Serum ADH is undetectable. She responds well to intranasal desmopressin. Underlying cause?
A) Posterior pituitary destruction by placenta
B) Placental vasopressinase degrades ADH ✓
C) V2 receptor downregulation in pregnancy
D) Plasma volume diluting ADH
E) AQP2 channel dysfunction
✅ B — Gestational DI
Placenta produces vasopressinase → degrades endogenous ADH. DDAVP works because it is structurally resistant to vasopressinase. Resolves after delivery.
⚠️ Trap: ADH is LOW (degraded) in gestational DI — helps distinguish from central DI, where ADH is absent due to production failure. Both respond to DDAVP.
⚡ HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY — Unit 4
  • ADH: supraoptic nuclei → posterior pituitary. Released by ↑osmolality (primary) or ↓volume
  • V1a = vasoconstriction (Gq). V1b = ↑ACTH (Gq). V2 = antidiuresis via AQP2 (Gs/cAMP)
  • DDAVP: V2-selective agonist, resistant to vasopressinase → treats central and gestational DI
  • Central DI: ↓ADH → dilute urine + hypernatremia → RESPONDS to DDAVP
  • Nephrogenic DI: kidney unresponsive → dilute urine + hypernatremia → does NOT respond to DDAVP
  • Gestational DI: placental vasopressinase → responds to DDAVP (resistant to vasopressinase)
  • Oxytocin: suckling → milk ejection. Uterine distension → parturition. Receptor ↑ at term

5.5 Thyroid Hormone Receptor Mechanism

WITHOUT T3 (TR on TRE)WITH T3 (TR on TRE)
TR bound to TRE as heterodimer with RXR. Recruits co-REPRESSORS → actively REPRESSES transcription T3 binds TR → co-repressors leave → co-ACTIVATORS recruited → ACTIVATES transcription of: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (↑metabolism), β1-AR (↑HR sensitivity), SERCA2 (↑Ca²⁺ cycling), myosin isoforms
🎯 HOW THIS IS TESTED — Unit 5
  • TSH interpretation is MOST TESTED: ↑TSH = primary hypothyroid | ↓TSH = hyperthyroid or central hypothyroid
  • Lab pattern: ↑TSH + ↓T4 = primary hypothyroid. ↓TSH + ↑T4 = primary hyperthyroid. ↓TSH + ↓T4 = secondary/central hypothyroid
  • Graves' vs Hashimoto's: BOTH autoimmune, BOTH can have anti-TPO. KEY DIFFERENCE: Graves = hyperthyroid + TSI; Hashimoto = hypothyroid + anti-TPO/-Tg
  • PTU unique: blocks TPO + blocks peripheral T4→T3 → preferred in thyroid storm and 1st trimester
  • Wolff-Chaikoff: used pre-op (Lugol's) or for storm; effect escapes after ~48 hours
⚠️ TEST TRAPS — Unit 5
  • Exophthalmos in Graves': NOT from T3/T4 — TSI activates TSH-R on ORBITAL FIBROBLASTS → GAG deposition → swelling. Persists after thyroidectomy!
  • PTU vs Methimazole pregnancy: PTU in 1st trimester (MMI = aplasia cutis). Switch to MMI in 2nd/3rd trimester
  • Sick euthyroid syndrome: ↑rT3, ↓T3, ↓T4, normal/↓TSH — DO NOT treat with T4
  • Hashitoxicosis: early Hashimoto's → follicular destruction → transient hyperthyroid phase before hypothyroid
  • Lithium inhibits T3/T4 RELEASE → can cause hypothyroidism/goiter
  • Pendred syndrome: deafness + goiter — pendrin mutation (iodide can't enter colloid despite normal NIS uptake)
📝 MINI Q 9
A 32-year-old pregnant woman (8 weeks) has Graves' disease and requires antithyroid drug therapy. Most appropriate choice?
A) Methimazole — once daily dosing preferred
B) Radioactive iodine — safest in young women
C) PTU — also inhibits peripheral T4→T3 conversion ✓
D) Perchlorate — blocks iodide uptake without crossing placenta
E) Lugol's iodine — Wolff-Chaikoff provides long-term control
✅ C — PTU in 1st trimester
Methimazole causes aplasia cutis and choanal atresia in 1st trimester. PTU preferred + blocks T4→T3 conversion. After 1st trimester, switch to methimazole (PTU hepatotoxicity risk).
⚠️ Trap: I-131 ABSOLUTELY contraindicated in pregnancy. Lugol's effect only transient (Wolff-Chaikoff escapes after 48h).
📝 MINI Q 10
A 55-year-old woman has fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation. TSH 12 mIU/L, T4 3.2 μg/dL, anti-TPO antibodies positive. Most specific finding confirming the diagnosis?
A) Elevated TSH alone
B) Low T4 and T3
C) Positive anti-TPO antibodies
D) Combination of ↑TSH + ↓T4 with positive anti-TPO ✓
E) Low radioactive iodine uptake on scan
✅ D — ↑TSH + ↓T4 + anti-TPO
Hashimoto's = autoimmune primary hypothyroidism. ↑TSH + ↓T4/T3 confirms primary hypothyroid. Anti-TPO antibodies + hypothyroid labs = Hashimoto's specifically.
⚠️ Trap: Graves' ALSO has anti-TPO antibodies in ~70% of cases — antibody positivity alone doesn't distinguish. Graves' = hyperthyroid labs + TSI. Hashimoto's = hypothyroid labs + anti-TPO/-Tg.
⚡ HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY — Unit 5
  • Synthesis: NIS → Pendrin → TPO (oxidation + organification + coupling) → Tg storage → TSH-stimulated release → deiodinase (T4→T3)
  • T4 = prohormone (more secreted, longer t½). T3 = active form (10x more potent, shorter t½)
  • PTU: blocks TPO + T4→T3. Methimazole: blocks TPO only. PTU preferred in 1st trimester + thyroid storm
  • Graves' = ↓TSH + ↑T4/T3 + TSI + exophthalmos (orbital fibroblast activation)
  • Hashimoto's = ↑TSH + ↓T4/T3 + anti-TPO/anti-Tg. Most common US hypothyroidism
  • Wolff-Chaikoff: excess iodide blocks TPO → used pre-op/storm. Escapes after 48h
  • TR: bound to DNA without hormone = REPRESSES. T3 binding = ACTIVATES. RXR heterodimer
